# SHE KNOWS THE WAY

Creative replacement for the rejected card-based campaign. Fictional short story; not a reenactment of the founder or his daughter. Paid rendering is authorized; final creative acceptance and campaign launch remain pending.

## Story

The Town has gone quiet. A man notices a small figure who seems to know where everyone is going. Skyy offers an invitation with her hand. He follows. Other faces awaken across the collection worlds. The Town Line arrives; its light connects them. At the destination, Skyy turns back once before the House opens.

The emotional progression is curiosity → recognition → movement → belonging. The relaunch becomes the opening of a place these people have been making their way toward.

## Performance and source rules

The models are the people already in the product cards. No generic replacement cast. BR-004 keeps the silver botanical rose/cloud graphic at its original relative scale. SG-009 keeps the open black shell and white lining; no added hood or reversible interpretation. LH-004 remains hood-up and front-facing; do not invent back artwork, reverse lettering, or open the jacket. Kids-001 uses kids-001-onmodel-v2.webp, preserving age, panel colors, emblems and single sleeve patch.

Skyy uses the visually inspected skyy-mascot Element cf20a690-7828-4738-9557-2ef00bc42591, in its original white/black Love Hurts outfit. It remains a stylized animated character. Do not apply human garments to Skyy, transfer Skyy's lettering to a model, or make the photographic child into the mascot.

Generated frames must be compared with source faces, silhouettes and garment marks. A strong prompt is not proof of fidelity. Reject identity swaps, invented graphics, drifting emblems, wrong letters, extra fingers or inconsistent scale. Avoid unseen backs and large spins. Create movement through expression, weight, measured steps, fabric response and camera travel.

## Enhanced motion prompt: encounter pilot

Use the selected encounter keyframe as the first frame. One continuous five-second cinematic shot. The same adult BR-004 model notices Skyy; his eyes and chin lift subtly, then he transfers his weight into one measured forward step, chest staying toward camera. Skyy gives a small beckoning gesture with her existing extended hand and glances toward the distant warm light. Keep both characters separate and maintain their exact faces, body proportions and original outfits. The human remains photographic; Skyy remains stylized 3D. Dolly slowly to the right by roughly half a meter to create parallax around the concrete pillar. Water reflections shift naturally, with restrained fabric movement. No cuts, face morphs, costume changes, graphic animation, lip-sync, new people, back views or camera orbit. Finish with a short hold on their decision to move. Sound: distant rails, soft wet footfall and wind; no spoken words.
