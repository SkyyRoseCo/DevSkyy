Claude's terminal showed a second research round in progress; this report evaluates the written snapshot, not unfinished work. Claude's source documents were not edited.

## Judgment

The research is a useful reference library with unusually candid evidence limitations. Its strongest contribution is connecting a stable brand meaning, observable product evidence, production planning, and purchase readiness. It is not yet a dependable SkyyRose directing or launch specification.

The most consequential problem is the application section: main report lines153–163 evaluates the old `strategy.md`. That plan predates Kids-first, the current story boards, and the protected original Black Rose footage. Correcting citations alone will not correct this mismatch.

The useful next step is to translate selected findings into the current treatment and shot packet, while keeping vendor recipes and unverified performance claims out of execution rules. Citation quantity establishes discovery breadth; it does not establish causal evidence or successful authenticated execution.

## Corrections ranked by decision impact

**1. Rebase the SkyyRose application on the current brief.** Main line155 describes environment-only generation, unchanged product panels, 26 references and 1080×1920 masters. Our current task introduces Skyy in Kids, requires causal character encounters, and preserves the actual Black Rose performer and original video excerpts. The original footage is native 720×1280 at 24000/1001fps. A universal 1080 master or whole-film grade must not silently alter it. Line168's “one grade” formulation also conflicts with this treatment. Match newly generated coverage to the source where feasible; do not modify protected excerpts to conceal generated identity drift.

**2. Repair the model-capability claims.** Storyboard ledger line27 attributes Motion Brush to a Gen-4 production, but Runway documents it as a Gen-2-only feature unavailable in newer models. Gen-2 was deprecated May 11, 2025. Line25 also conflates Gen-4 Image named references with general video-reference controls. The official image workflow allows up to three active references; it does not prove an arbitrary video connector accepts or binds those references. Specify provider, model/version, interface, production stage and supported input fields separately. Sources: [Runway Gen-2 Deprecation](https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/41072248471187-Gen-2-Deprecation), [Gen-4 Image References](https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/40042718905875-Creating-with-Gen-4-Image-References).

**3. Keep anecdotal production advice out of mandatory rules.** Fixed shot counts, a 3% stills-cost ratio, 75–85% rejection rates, a 48-hour turnaround, last-frame chaining and “approve stills, not clips” describe particular workflows. They are not universal operating standards. Approve composition in stills, then separately inspect motion, performance, identity, garments and edit continuity. Recheck chained frames against canonical sources so drift does not accumulate.

**4. Restore denominators and measurement definitions.** The Top Growth sample for site conversion contains 19 stores; its paid ROAS samples contain only 5 Meta and 3 Google accounts. These are platform-attributed results, not incremental returns caused by a campaign. Klaviyo's reported 41% email revenue from 5.3% of sends is supported by its own benchmark page, but flows and broadcasts reach people with different intent. Neither statistic predicts our sales. Sources: [Top Growth methodology](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/dtc-benchmarks-2026/), [Klaviyo benchmarks](https://www.klaviyo.com/products/email-marketing/benchmarks).

**5. Separate production lead time from the seven-day release schedule.** Main lines99–112 and156 treat lengthy launch templates as a norm against the old campaign. Three weeks of anticipation or eight-to-twelve weeks of preparation do not establish that a seven-day publishing sequence is wrong. Preserve preparation, publication and learning as distinct schedules. Budget percentages, simultaneous email/SMS, gated shopping and deadline messages require actual operating facts; they should not be inherited from an agency article.

**6. Correct smaller factual errors that weaken confidence.** Oaklandish's current official About page states at least 2% annually, rather than an unqualified 10% of revenue. Historical 10% reporting needs its period attached. The heritage paper is in the *Journal of Advertising Research*; its abstract does not establish a universal apparel-sales rule. The storyboard ledger lists 94 unique URL rows while claiming 96. Sources: [Oaklandish](https://www.oaklandish.com/pages/about-us), [publisher abstract](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00218499.2026.2648909).

**7. Narrow legal claims to the actual rule and asset.** Main line145 overgeneralizes disclosure. The European Commission FAQ describes cumulative deepfake criteria and a distinct disclosure treatment for evidently artistic or fictional work; it also calls the Code voluntary. This does not establish that every generated fantasy scene requires identical burned-in labels. Conversely, the reported New York June 9, 2026 effective date is confirmed by the governor's announcement. Classify the intended asset and placement using primary rules before deciding implementation; this review is not legal clearance. Sources: [Commission FAQ](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act), [Article 50](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-50), [New York announcement](https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/governor-hochul-announces-first-nation-law-requiring-disclosure-when-advertisements-include-ai).

**8. Do not infer missing site safeguards from an old campaign file.** Line162's statement that migration controls are absent is not a fresh audit of V2's implementation or release evidence. Inspect the relevant current files and deployment evidence before calling a safeguard missing or complete.

## What this reveals about our own directing work

The same specificity test must apply to our Kids Route A. The imitation exchange is a promising way to establish Skyy as a participant: she responds, initiates, and creates room for the other child to respond. Its current schematic board addresses causal readability. It does not yet prove that the event belongs uniquely to SkyyRose, that viewers desire the clothes, or that the eventual film will feel premium.

A readable game can still be generic. A train can supply location without supplying plot. A collection hero can decorate the background without affecting action. We should not add more spectacle and assume those problems disappear.

Use separate review questions:

1. **Story:** What did each character do that caused the next response? What changed?
2. **Character:** What did viewers learn about Skyy beyond her appearance? Is that behavior consistent when she enters adult chapters?
3. **Brand:** Could the same event, wardrobe and world be assigned to another brand without meaningful revision?
4. **Product:** Which exact garment and identifying detail do viewers remember? Is the source model and wardrobe intact?
5. **Desire and purchase confidence:** Do relevant adult buyers want to learn more about or wear/buy the product, and can its destination answer fit, availability and delivery questions?

A small qualitative retell test can reveal confusion. “Four of five understood it” is not a validated sales threshold. An agent review is not a target-customer study. Creative acceptance remains distinct from technical export completion.

## Example of stronger application

**Evidence-backed principle, proposed application:** the research's specificity and retelling methods support changing the shot packet from “cinematic train reveal, luxury lighting, same mascot” into observable action. For the current Kids route, specify who initiates each move, whose gaze communicates that initiation, the time needed to recognize the response, and which garment features remain legible. Review with notes hidden. This is a proposed test, not a newly observed audience result.

**Illustrative incorrect application:** “Use 15–20 shots, an expensive lens look, a common LUT and a large reveal; five reviewers liked it, so render all collections and launch.” The correction is to establish causal clarity, brand specificity and source fidelity first, then test one complete exchange in motion. A grading recipe cannot repair the wrong performer, and approval of a rough board cannot stand in for acceptance of generated footage.

## Recommended next directing sequence

1. Attach a concise current brief to the research: Kids-first; canonical Skyy; exact product/model references; wordless films; protected Black Rose insert rules; separate collection stories and main edit.
2. Compare genuinely different story mechanisms using the same brand and garment truth. Change the decision and consequence, not merely the carriage decor or camera move.
3. Review silent boards for the five questions above. Preserve recorded observations separately from creator intent and audience hypotheses.
4. Qualify the exact still-to-video route, then produce one complete, source-faithful exchange for visual review before scaling production.
5. Derive each accepted scene's film, product and placement assets; verify its actual shopping destination and measurement before any release.

This is a recommendation from the review. It does not replace the current treatment, approve new media, or initiate a campaign.
