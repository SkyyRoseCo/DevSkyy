# SkyyRose Film Production Playbook

## 1. Production approach

The recommended workflow is to establish the story and source identities, approve the scene composition, generate a bounded action, and assemble the film around verified coverage. Prompt enhancement belongs between a directing decision and a generation request. Its purpose is to make the decision executable without changing the cast, garment, action or reveal.

The campaign consists of collection shorts connected by a train journey and a main commercial culminating in the Kids line. Each short needs an intelligible local event. The combined film needs an opening question, escalation and a payoff of its own. An attractive sequence of locations does not establish those relationships. These are directing recommendations for this campaign, not a claim that every fashion advertisement requires the same narrative structure.

The Academy's animation guide treats boards as a way to plan action and discover problems before animation. Adobe's shot-list guidance connects blocking and camera planning with the coverage an editor needs. Together, these support moving structural decisions ahead of expensive motion production.[1][2]

**Production sequence:** source brief → competing premises → blocking and timed boards → reference-bound stills → reviewed motion → source-preserving assembly → independent review → final creative acceptance.

| Workstream | Required output | Decision owner |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | Character objective, event, choice, consequence, reveal | Director |
| Brand and product | Current SKU, approved cast, mascot and hero references | Source/fidelity owner |
| Image preparation | Approved shot composition and reference roles | Image lead and director |
| Motion | Action, timing, camera behavior and continuity | Motion lead |
| Assembly | Selected coverage, protected excerpts and cut relationships | Editor |
| Review | Specific findings against the actual candidate | Independent reviewer and founder |

A successful generation is a candidate, not a completed film. No provider's marketing language establishes that an exact face, embroidered mark or garment construction will remain correct across all frames. High visual quality, story comprehension and source preservation are separate requirements.

**Current working recommendation, September 12:** use the [research-integrated directing sequence](../directing-workflow/NEXT-DIRECTING-SEQUENCE.md). Kids is the first complete story proof. Qualify one complete exchange using the established Kids product-card identities, canonical illustrated Skyy and readable verified Kids garments before scaling production. Qualify the actual Black Rose performer, br-006 and protected excerpts separately. An unrelated beauty shot cannot qualify an interaction workflow.

## 2. Source and identity contract

Black Rose features br-006 and the actual male performer in the supplied April 2024 video. The supplied image.png controls Skyy's recurring illustrated identity. Collection archive images describe wardrobe; they do not authorize a different mascot face. Other human performers remain tied to their approved product-card sources. These requirements are recorded in the campaign's source and directing records.[3]

Keep four distinct reference roles: identity, garment, environment and composition. A garment photo cannot establish a performer's identity, and a collection hero cannot establish a SKU. An accepted generated frame may serve as a composition or motion starting point, while canonical sources remain the authority used to inspect it.

| Binding | Required record | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|
| Human identity | Exact source frames, hash and approved performer | Reusing an attractive invented model |
| Skyy identity | Supplied master image and distinguishing proportions | Letting an archive outfit redefine the face |
| Product | Current SKU, verified views and construction details | Assuming the name “sherpa” proves visible cream panels |
| Collection hero | Exact hero asset and the feature being adapted | Copying a stale product assignment from its manifest |
| Composition | Approved still with shot ID and version | Treating the image as new product truth |
| Remote reference | Actual uploaded asset/Element and local-source mapping | Mentioning a filename without attaching media |

Record conflicts rather than silently resolving them by recency. The older train manifest's Black Rose product assignment differs from the current br-006 requirement. Current exact-decoded performer images also differ from earlier converted upload files. A hash establishes byte identity; it does not establish visual correctness or current approval.[3]

For new actions, choose an explicit route: an approved source already showing the pose; protected-layer/verified 3D treatment; or a generative candidate within the existing authorization for new scenes. A directing proposal is not proof of exact-product preservation. Preserve applicable authorization already given and identify only the actual uncovered change.[4]

**Identity review:** inspect the human's facial proportions and hair separately from Skyy's face, curls, eye shape, scale and illustrated rendering. Inspect wardrobe independently of both. Realistic lighting on Skyy should integrate her with the scene while preserving her illustrated design. Converting her into a photographic child would violate the identity brief.

## 3. Story and reveal development

Write a premise in observable terms: a character is trying to do something; a visible event interrupts or complicates it; a choice changes what happens next. The ending may resolve the attempt or change the audience's understanding. Mystery can delay an answer, but it must provide a question worth following.

Develop three substantially different mechanisms before polishing one. A different lens, carriage or weather condition is a visual variation. A different objective, relationship or consequence is a story alternative. The train must affect what can happen: movement, windows, connected spaces, restricted sightlines or the timing of an arrival should matter to the event.

The Science Behind Pixar's story-and-art presentation shows timed drawings used to refine plot, pacing and camera decisions. Its distinction between storyboards, concept art and model packets is useful here: atmosphere, action and identity require different artifacts.[5]

**Director's development prompt — illustrative, unexecuted:**

> Develop three wordless Black Rose premises using the supplied performer, canonical Skyy and br-006 aboard the train. Give each a different visible objective and causal mechanism. For each, state the opening question, the action that changes the situation, the ending, the product-reading moment and the collection-hero feature. Explain why the train matters. Identify the exact beat most likely to feel contrived. Do not invent accepted cast relationships, unseen source footage or garment features. Return development proposals, not generation prompts.

**Weak direction:** “Skyy appears, smiles, points, and the model follows to a huge reveal.” The gesture moves the cast but does not establish why the model follows or what the reveal resolves. A purposeful encounter needs more than recognition of matching outfits.

**Stronger development question:** “What can the model understand or accomplish after meeting Skyy that was unavailable before?” This permits discovery, rivalry, an exchange, a reversal or shared action. It does not require Skyy to become a tour guide in every collection.

For each reveal, record: what is hidden; the clue that precedes it; the audience's likely interpretation; the newly visible information; and the consequence. Large architecture, an expansive view or an ambitious camera move may be appropriate. Scale alone is not an answer to the opening question.

For the main film, create a matrix of opening clues, local payoffs and contributions to the Kids reveal. Avoid repeating the same invitation in four costumes. The Kids chapter needs an action of its own and recognizable clothing, not only a final posed group image.

## 4. Blocking and camera design

Draw the carriage before choosing lens language. Place both characters, windows, doors, camera positions and the relevant hero feature. Mark where each person looks, which way they travel, what their hands touch and which garment surfaces remain visible. A reflection needs a plausible relationship between object, reflective surface, viewer and camera.

Adobe describes the 180-degree system as a way to maintain understandable spatial relationships. Apply it as a continuity aid, with an intentional visible transition when the camera crosses the established axis. It is not a prohibition on expressive filmmaking.[6]

Higgsfield's camera guide distinguishes text direction from explicit Cinema Studio settings. Its practical recommendation to specify movement and final framing is useful. Claims that settings make physical behavior identical on every run should be treated as vendor assertions, not deterministic guarantees.[7]

| Intended effect | Directing instruction | Product implication |
|---|---|---|
| Recognition | Let the eyeline change before the body follows | Preserve face visibility long enough to read the decision |
| Approach | Track toward an established destination | Do not let foreground objects hide every identifying detail |
| Reveal | Move from a defined obstruction to a defined final view | Schedule separate garment coverage if the reveal faces away |
| Inspection | Hold a deliberate front or detail composition | Allow readable construction and authentic markings |
| Spectacle | Establish start position, path and landing frame | Check anatomy and identity through rapid changes |

**Camera example — illustrative, unexecuted:** “Begin behind the near window jamb with the monument outside the composition. Move laterally along the carriage interior until the jamb clears the view. Settle on the model and Skyy looking toward the now-visible monument.” This specifies the mechanism of concealment and disclosure. A board must still demonstrate the geography.

Do not combine a fast full orbit with a requirement that two stationary jacket fronts remain visible throughout. Decide whether the shot's job is complete circumferential coverage, an interaction, or a front-reading interval. Divide incompatible jobs into complementary coverage.

Specify camera controls once in the appropriate place. If the selected interface supplies a camera-style field, do not give it a static preset while asking for a dolly in the text. Focal length and aperture values are intended visual controls; they do not prove measured real-world behavior. Higgsfield itself describes amplified lens character and supports video-first generation. Approving a still first is the recommended fidelity checkpoint here, not a universal Cinema Studio requirement.[18]

## 5. Prompt enhancement procedure

Treat enhancement as a controlled translation with a visible change record. Preserve the directing intent and facts; expand only the information needed to express them in the selected image workflow. More adjectives are not a success measure.

The local Higgsfield photoshoot route documents separate intent, brand context, product context and reference inputs. Its installed skill variant differs from the local workflow, including the named image model and manual versus backend prompting. Select the route explicitly rather than merging incompatible instructions.[8]

| Step | Operation | Required result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Freeze the directing brief and source roles | Known facts separated from creative decisions and unknowns |
| 2 | State one visible instant | A still-image composition, not a complete sequence |
| 3 | Supply brand and product context | Canonical identity, current SKU and verified appearance |
| 4 | Attach actual references and explicit canvas | Correct media bindings and 9:16 where required |
| 5 | Obtain the expanded prompt through the supported route | Exposed expansion retained; unavailable text marked unverified |
| 6 | Compare expansion with intent and evidence | Added facts, omissions and contradictions identified |
| 7 | Resolve conflicts | Revised intent/context or a separately declared supported route |
| 8 | Inspect final submission | Text, references and settings checked; unavailable fields identified |

The historical generic diagnostic invented a flower, an overcoat, a blue mascot and carriage wording. The more specific diagnostic preserved more of the brief but still added anti-cartoon and anti-doll-eye instructions that conflicted with Skyy. Both omitted an aspect override and returned a 4:5 default. Neither test attached image references, so neither tested visual fidelity.[9]

**Enhancement reviewer prompt — illustrative, unexecuted:**

> Compare the expanded prompt with the directing brief and reference manifest. List every added factual claim, dropped requirement and contradiction. For each garment claim, identify the supporting reference or mark it unsupported. Check human and mascot identity separately. Check camera motion against settings, framing against readable product intervals, and canvas against delivery. Return ACCEPTABLE_FOR_STORYBOARD_REVIEW or REVISE with exact reasons. Do not infer uploaded media from filenames. Do not rewrite the approved story or claim a rendered result.

The documented enhance-only route does not establish a supported mechanism for freezing its expanded prompt into a later render. Record that as a handoff dependency. If submission enhances again, inspect the new expansion when exposed. If the submitted expansion is hidden, mark it unverified, retain the available request receipt and inspect the rendered candidate against canonical sources before acceptance. Do not claim the previously reviewed expansion was submitted unchanged. If direct generation is chosen, declare the different route and review its prompt and references under that route's contract; do not label it the unchanged photoshoot workflow.[8]

## 6. Still prompt and motion prompt separation

An image prompt establishes what is visible. A motion prompt describes the change from the approved starting frame. Runway's current image-to-video guidance specifically addresses Gen-4.5 and recommends focusing on subject, environment and camera movement rather than redundantly reconstructing the image. This is model-specific guidance, not a universal ban on detailed image prompts.[10]

**Storyboard intent — illustrative, unexecuted:**

> Create one vertical storyboard candidate for the approved Black Rose encounter. Use the supplied performer references for the photographic man and the supplied Skyy master for the illustrated mascot. Use the verified br-006 references for wardrobe. At this instant, Skyy has stopped at the opposite window; the man has just noticed her change in attention. Frame them in an oblique front two-shot with hands below the chest and enough separation to inspect both jacket fronts. Establish the aisle and the window she is watching. Lighting integrates photographic skin and the supplied illustrated character without redesigning either. Preserve the verified garment marks and construction. Keep the carriage free of added captions or invented lettering.

This is a provisional shot specification, not an accepted campaign premise. Before submitting it, resolve the approved pose route, actual reference attachments, composition and optical plan. Keep the identity image's Love Hurts outfit separate from the Black Rose wardrobe instruction.

**Motion instruction from an approved matching frame — illustrative, unexecuted:**

> Skyy turns her head toward the window and settles. The man notices her eyeline, then turns his head toward the same point. Their feet remain planted and their hands stay below their jacket fronts. The camera makes a short lateral move and settles into a steady two-shot. The movement is continuous and unhurried.

**Proposed settings:** five seconds, 9:16, audio off where supported. Allocate roughly the first three seconds to the sequential gaze changes and camera move, then two seconds to the settled product view. These are planning targets to verify in the actual clip.

Its starting frame must already support the described positions. A concise prompt cannot repair incorrect cast, crossed sightlines or an unreadable jacket in that frame. If the shot needs a walk across the aisle, prepare the appropriate start state and action coverage instead of extending this prompt with unrelated events.

Use positive motion instructions for models whose guidance favors them: “the camera remains fixed” rather than a long list of unwanted movements. Keep forbidden identity changes and product defects in the review contract even when they are not pasted into the motion text. A negative-prompt convention from one model should not be copied blindly to another.[10]

**Editor instruction:** select the accepted action interval, retain enough time to read the decision, and cut into the established next eyeline. Keep original-footage ranges and source-preservation requirements in the edit record. They are not image-generation instructions.

## 7. Provider route selection

Choose the route by the shot's input requirements and the controls available on the exact surface. “Runway” is a platform; a Runway-hosted Kling route and Runway Gen-4.5 are different generation paths. Native Kling, Higgsfield web controls, Higgsfield CLI and connector tools also have distinct contracts.

| Route | Recommended role | Qualification needed |
|---|---|---|
| Higgsfield photoshoot enhancement | Expand a reviewed still intent with explicit source context | Actual references, contradiction review and final-prompt handoff |
| Reference-based still generation | Establish a composition with the correct cast and garment | Independent still identity, product and optical review |
| Kling through a supported reference route | Candidate motion for recurring cast and product | Exact model, reference limits, accepted start frame and temporal review |
| Runway Gen-4.5 image-to-video | Candidate action from an approved composition | Motion prompt and model-specific capability check |
| Cinema Studio | Candidate camera-driven shot or structured sequence | Exact version, settings and interface parity |
| Automatic product-ad recipe | Rapid complete-ad exploration when its autonomy fits | Storyboard review behavior and scope before execution |
| Local deterministic editing | Preserve original excerpts and assemble accepted coverage | Metadata, source-frame comparison and viewing review |

Runway Gen-4 References is a still-image workflow with up to three references. It is not evidence that the same reference interface exists in every Runway video model.[11] Kling's native documentation distinguishes Video 3.0 from Video 3.0 Omni and describes reference and multi-shot features by route. Those features support conditioning; they do not establish a zero-drift guarantee.[12]

**Research correction:** exclude Motion Brush from any Gen-4 route specification. Runway documents it as a Gen-2 feature unavailable on newer models; Gen-2 was deprecated May 11, 2025. Reference capacities and named controls must match the exact interface used. [Official deprecation documentation](https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/41072248471187-Gen-2-Deprecation). Review both stills and resulting motion; a correct still does not prove temporal fidelity.

The public Higgsfield CLI schema documents Cinema Studio 3.5 video controls, including style-related settings, start/end references and multi-shot input. It also identifies mutually exclusive ways of supplying style. Website documentation describing a newer Cinema Studio release must not silently replace the schema of an older CLI or connector model.[13]

Runway's Product Ad recipe analyzes product images, creates a storyboard and renders a video as an execution workflow. It is not a harmless prompt-enhancement call. Its documented version can be pinned, but its current documentation does not establish the separate storyboard-approval checkpoint required for this campaign.[14]

**Recommendation:** retain Higgsfield and Runway/Kling as candidate production routes, then qualify the chosen route against the actual interaction. No comparative campaign test currently establishes a universal highest-quality model. Account access, supported parameters and price must be checked when the production request is concrete; old balances are not a current quote.

## 8. Reference continuity across shots

Build an approved source manifest before any series-wide generation. Attach identity and wardrobe sources in their supported roles, then inspect the generated composition. Where the model accepts reusable Elements, bind the actual selected Element and verify that its contents correspond to the intended source. A display name alone is insufficient.

Use a shot-state record with entry and exit positions, screen direction, costume, relative scale, hand occupancy, gaze target and lighting direction. The next shot must either continue that state or show an intelligible transition. Different collection outfits are deliberate chapter changes; face and body identity should remain stable.

**Reference handoff example — illustrative, unexecuted:**

> Actor identity: exact decoded performer frames 731 and 1427. Mascot identity: supplied image.png. Wardrobe: current br-006 garment references and verified Black Rose Skyy outfit reference. Environment: Black Rose hero feature only. Composition: accepted shot BR-03 version 2. Motion start: its approved image hash. No rejected generated face is an identity reference.

Before applying this example, resolve the actual assets and approval state. The example labels are not upload IDs, and the proposed shot version is not a claim that such a board exists. Never place invented reference identifiers into a provider request.

Start and end frames can constrain endpoints when supported. They do not prove that the route between them preserves identity, hands, garment marks or spatial logic. Inspect crossings, occlusions and turns especially closely. A face can return to the right appearance at the last frame after drifting in the middle.[4][12]

When choosing between individual shots and a multi-shot request, decide whether the benefit is coordinated sequence generation or easier isolation of errors. Multi-shot output still needs shot-by-shot and cross-cut review. Splitting a failed sequence is often more informative than regenerating every shot with a longer prompt.

**Failure recovery:** classify the defect first. Wrong identity requires reference/composition correction. Unsupported garment construction requires a source or fidelity-route correction. Confusing action requires blocking or story revision. A failed camera move requires an explicit path/settings revision. A weak edit may require only different trims or shot order. Preserve the accepted decisions that do not depend on the failed part.

## 9. Original footage and commercial assembly

The supplied Black Rose video remains the source of the actual performer and product. Excerpts are permitted; source audio is excluded. Protected excerpts retain their visual content. Do not use generative restyling, face substitution, crop, retiming, grading or upscaling to make them fit a generated scene.[3][4]

Place source footage where its real action makes sense. A product insert may introduce the garment, provide a deliberate inspection interval or punctuate a chapter. It should not imply that the original man performed a new train interaction absent from the footage. Choose the surrounding coverage and cut relationships accordingly.

The historical record identifies the 720 × 1280 source and specific frame ranges, but every new assembly needs its own comparison. A preservation master should retain native source geometry and time base, with generated coverage adapted around it. If a different canvas uses native-size padding, compare the extracted source region rather than claiming full-frame identity. A compressed viewing copy and a lossless preservation master have different verification claims.[3]

**Editor handoff — illustrative, unexecuted:**

> Bind the unchanged source file and approved frame ranges. Exclude its audio. Retain source order within each selected passage. Place generated shots before and after the passage using the established eyeline or product-detail relationship. Record any padding separately from source pixels. Compare every protected frame in the preservation master with its mapped source frame. Report the viewing encode's limits separately.

Commercial readability needs intentional screen time, framing and focus. A nominal two-second front interval is only a planning target; its adequacy depends on viewing size, movement and garment detail. Test the actual edit on a phone-sized presentation. Verify that the intended bomber is recognizable, not merely that a dark jacket appears.

Each collection's hero feature should be identified in the production record and serve the composition or reveal. The train may connect environments without requiring an identical reveal pattern in every chapter. For the main commercial, select the minimum coverage that preserves each chapter's contribution and earns the Kids payoff. Reusing all four shorts in full is not required.

## 10. Evaluation and controlled iteration

Evaluate story, performance, identity, product, optics, motion and editing separately. VBench-2.0 distinguishes human fidelity, controllability, creativity, physics and commonsense. That separation supports a multidimensional review instead of a single “cinematic quality” score.[15]

Automated judgments remain supporting evidence. The WorldJen preprint criticizes several benchmark limitations, including missed temporal failures and binary-question bias, and evaluates an alternative against a bounded human study. Its results do not validate a general-purpose judge for SkyyRose garments. Treat machine similarity as triage and retain native-detail visual inspection.[16]

| Review | Reviewer task | Failure response |
|---|---|---|
| Silent story | Describe events, objective, change and ending without the treatment | Revise boards or story; do not buy polish |
| Engagement | Identify the moment attention weakened and why | Rework pacing, uncertainty or payoff |
| Human and Skyy identity | Compare each separately with canonical sources | Reject changed identity; correct bindings |
| Garment | Inspect marks, closures, panels, fit and silhouette | Reject drift or unsupported construction |
| Spatial continuity | Track gaze, travel, contact and reflection geography | Repair blocking or coverage |
| Motion | Inspect turns, occlusions, hands and transitions | Simplify or split the failed action |
| Product comprehension | Identify the product after phone-sized playback | Improve framing, focus or interval |
| Protected footage | Compare all mapped original frames and audio policy | Repair assembly and rerun comparison |

A fresh story reviewer should see untitled silent boards before receiving the explanation. Collect free-response observations first, then ask targeted questions. A rough drawing that cannot communicate a gesture is insufficient evidence; do not call it a story pass merely because the prose is clear.

**Proposed qualification experiment, not an executed benchmark:** compare two supported routes on the same approved start frame and one action. Hold duration, canvas and intended framing as close as the routes permit. Vary one prompt or control decision at a time. Record failures as well as selected outputs. If only one sample per route is affordable, describe the result as exploratory rather than a reliable model ranking.

A proposed scorecard can use 0–2 ratings for story, action, product readability, camera intent and continuity, but wrong casting, altered product identity or changed protected footage are rejection conditions regardless of the total. These are campaign-specific recommendations, not validated numerical thresholds.

## 11. Adoption and production packet

The existing directing skill covers causal story, source roles, blocking, protected footage and independent review. The practical additions below turn research into a repeatable production handoff. They are recommended changes to adopt deliberately; the research playbook does not itself rewrite the installed provider skills.[17]

1. **Add an enhancement change record.** Compare intent with expansion and final submission. Name additions, omissions and contradictions instead of rewarding prompt length.
2. **Record a provider contract per shot.** Include platform, model/version, interface, supported reference roles, actual media IDs, canvas, timing, audio policy and settings. Record when the schema was checked.
3. **Split the handoff into three instructions.** The still describes a moment; motion describes change; the edit describes selected intervals and relationships.
4. **Use a source-role matrix.** Resolve stale SKU assignments, local-versus-uploaded bytes and archive-wardrobe versus master-identity conflicts.
5. **Review silent boards before motion.** Keep story comprehension and product recognition separate from visual polish.
6. **Keep a bounded qualification record.** State what the candidate demonstrated, what failed and what remains untested before scaling.

**Reusable production packet**

| Field group | Minimum contents |
|---|---|
| Directing | Opening question, visible objective, beat, consequence, reveal relationship |
| Sources | Identity, wardrobe, hero and composition bindings; conflict resolutions |
| Staging | Entry/exit states, eyelines, direction, relative scale, contacts |
| Image | Still intent, brand/product context, expanded prompt, reviewed composition |
| Motion | Action sequence, camera path, accepted start/end states where supported |
| Delivery | Explicit canvas, timing, audio, protected-source geometry and encode policy |
| Evidence | Request receipt, candidate hash, review findings and actual acceptance states |

All new prompt examples in this playbook are illustrative and unexecuted. Historical enhancement responses are observed records; they are not fresh account or rendering evidence. Offline document and source review requires no provider authentication. Current authenticated production, successful reference injection, motion fidelity and final campaign acceptance remain separate evidence requirements.

The first production milestone is a concrete, source-bound scene whose silent boards communicate the intended event. The next is an accepted motion proof that preserves the actual cast and garment. Only then should the workflow scale across collection coverage and the main edit.
