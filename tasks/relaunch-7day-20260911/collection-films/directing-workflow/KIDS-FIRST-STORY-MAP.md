## Campaign arc

Recommend Kids → Signature → Love Hurts → Black Rose → main commercial. The audience first discovers Skyy's personality, then sees different sides of her through encounters with established product-card models. Black Rose retains the actual man in the supplied video. The train connects the encounters. A newcomer must still understand each standalone without having seen the Kids debut.

Skyy's debut is a character reveal. Subsequent reveals concern choices, relationships, geography or a shared destination. Do not repeat a hidden mascot, an opening door and a giant landmark in every chapter. Do not spend the main commercial pretending Skyy is still unknown.

Working connective mechanism: **Your Move**. A character acts; the other responds unexpectedly; their relationship changes. This is an internal development label, not on-screen wording. Avoid turning every story into the same competitive posing game.

Alternative considered: **Before Anyone Sees**, four private preparations for a train-bound fashion presentation. This offers a concrete final destination, but risks a generic backstage montage and unsupported additional casting. Keep as an alternative, not established campaign lore.

## 1. Kids Capsule — She Moved First

**Question:** Who is copying whom?

**Proposed 20–25 second arc:** A real Kids product-card model establishes a composed stance in the observation carriage. The outfit is readable before Skyy's identity is fully shown. A second movement at the edge of the frame repeats the child's stance. Widen to reveal the supplied illustrated Skyy, in a source-matched Kids outfit. The child deliberately changes the stance to test her. Skyy initially follows, then makes a different, restrained movement of her own. The child responds to Skyy this time. The change in who leads resolves the opening question.

The final composition makes room for the second real Kids product-card model and the other colorway. Plant that second child's presence earlier through a readable partial composition so the ending is an ensemble discovery, not a random cast addition. The train's observation space expands into view; adapt a verified architectural shape from the Kids throne hero as set vocabulary. No coronation, sparkles, exaggerated reactions or invented costumes.

**What Skyy establishes:** observant, self-possessed, playful; she can initiate an action instead of merely imitate or guide.

**Product:** kids-001 and kids-002 are the existing source-map candidates. One persistent Skyy identity and one outfit within the continuous scene. The second child shows the other colorway; do not recolor Skyy mid-shot or spawn a second Skyy. Source references must resolve the exact movement: no invented shoe branding or accessories.

**Development test:** Try three mechanisms in rough boards: imitation becomes initiative (recommended); a deliberately interrupted solo portrait becomes a shared composition; the characters pursue the same view from different positions and negotiate it. These are different candidate actions, not three render requests. The recommended version is potentially too slight; if it reads as a pose loop, strengthen its reversal before polishing it.

## 2. Signature — At Her Pace

**Question:** Can two people with different rhythms move together without one simply following?

**Proposed arc:** The canonical Signature model moves deliberately through the carriage; Skyy, established as the initiator in Kids, expects him to match her quicker rhythm. He continues on his own timing. She stops checking whether he is following and watches what he is responding to: the moving train's changing view. They independently adjust and arrive together at a deliberately chosen observation position as the structure outside clears.

**Payoff to develop:** their independently chosen timing produces the shared view. The expansive city composition is earned through movement, not a random end-of-film monument. Use the source gold rose/metal curve as part of the final spatial composition, with both garment fronts readable.

**Product:** sg-009 with its canonical product-card model; distinguish its source construction from br-006. **Skyy's role:** learns another person's rhythm. **Open weakness:** the reason to choose this position and timing must be visible in the opening. Otherwise this is just two people walking and looking out of a window. Do not advance without solving that board-level problem.

## 3. Love Hurts — Meet Me Halfway

**Question:** Who will make the first move when neither wants to expose that they want company?

**Proposed arc:** The canonical Love Hurts model and Skyy occupy opposite sides of a glass partition inside the same carriage. Both approach a shared view, then withdraw when they notice the other. One starts toward the point where the partition ends and pauses; the other notices and makes the corresponding commitment. They meet in the open space beyond it. The camera reveals that each had been making room for the other.

This is mutual consideration between an adult and Skyy, not romance or an invented parent-child relationship. No second adult is added to solve the scene. The action must go beyond a smile, held door or offered seat. Keep the adult's visible decision central; Skyy cannot solve everything by pointing.

**Hero:** adapt the verified arch and glass-protected rose into the partition/view geography. Keep the rose intact; no shattered glass or invented symbolism of a deceased relative. **Product:** lh-004 and its canonical model, readable white/black construction without a red wash changing its color.

**Open weakness:** approach/withdrawal can become confusing or sentimental. Draw the floor plan and test whether a reviewer can identify both choices without reading this paragraph. If not, replace the mechanism rather than add explanatory imagery.

## 4. Black Rose — Unmoved

**Question:** What happens when Skyy's confidence meets someone who does not need to compete?

**Proposed arc:** Establish the actual man and bomber with a readable original-video excerpt. In newly created train coverage, Skyy attempts to draw him into the active exchange she has initiated in earlier films. He does not imitate her. She pauses and changes her approach; he then chooses a concise response on his own terms. The final image gives them equal presence, without making the adult a comic foil or Skyy an accessory.

This is the least resolved premise in R1. 'A concise response' is not yet a playable shot instruction. Develop concrete alternatives against the actual available source angles before choosing the interaction. Avoid replacing story with two people being cool. No claim that the supplied footage contains a reaction, glance or gesture that has not been inspected.

**Product and performer:** br-006, actual source-video man only. Archive bomber Skyy is wardrobe evidence; supplied image.png remains character identity. Original footage is a full-frame editorial insert showing the actual product, not footage of this fictional train event. New coverage must accommodate its appearance. Keep excerpts silent and visually unchanged; no crop, grade, retime, upscale, overlay or regeneration.

**Hero:** Bay Bridge structure and verified silver rose/star inform occlusion, light and composition. Do not end with the same monument zoom-out as the rejected package. **Development requirement:** explicitly solve his consequential response and the original-to-generated edit before motion production.

## Main commercial — the encounters converge

Recommend a new 40–60 second assembly, timing provisional. Introduce Skyy early using the Kids setup and decisive reversal so first-time viewers understand her. Intercut the later choices as the journey progresses. Give each collection a readable product interval. Build toward a new final shared composition or destination connecting the people already introduced; the exact destination remains a creative decision.
