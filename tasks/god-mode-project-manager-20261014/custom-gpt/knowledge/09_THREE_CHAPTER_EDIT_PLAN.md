## Chapter 1 — Black Rose / Before We Go

Objective: get ready and leave together while retaining individual style. Environment: lived-in apartment, shared window light, doorway and landing established. Age-appropriate Black adult/teen casts, separately male and female. The teen girl's approved charcoal-denim/sneakerhead styling remains. Do not present them as actual relatives or fictionalize founder biography.

B1 — “I want you to feel proud of who you are.” Medium eye-level view: a wearer checks their reflection, then looks toward a friend. Establish the window and exit; no floating isolated product. Opening should contain a face and readable garment, not only anonymous hands.
B2 — “Black is beautiful.” Hold on a different person's face and upper body in soft window light. Stillness is enough; no forced grin or model stare demanded.
B3 — “And I want you to feel that when you put this on.” A friend indicates a twisted sleeve; wearer finishes the adjustment. One action, clear hand ownership, no logo occlusion during the readable hold.
B4 — “Wear it how you wear it. Bring your own style to it.” Two or three distinct medium/full-body portraits inside the continuing preparation: seated sneaker finish, standing reflection, ready person by the doorway. Each retains their own silhouette; no uniform choreographed gestures.
B5 — “I want you to look in the mirror… and feel good about who you see.” Return to the opening wearer; their posture settles, then attention shifts from reflection to the friend waiting at the door. Confidence is observable ease, not a transformation effect.
B6 — Pause/payoff: ready friend waits instead of rushing; the last person joins and they leave. Male variation: wait on established landing. Female variation: sit beside the person finishing laces, then rise together. Neither ends in a group lineup.

Coverage target: 2–3 uninterrupted readable seconds for each of the five jerseys in each full Black Rose variant. Bind individual shot/wardrobe once casts and source imagery are reviewed. Do not claim a seconds-long abbreviated opening meets all-product coverage. Keep handles for flexible VO timing without duplicate-person composites.

Transition B→S: a departing wearer briefly crosses close to lens; motivated cut on the occlusion to a new Signature subject entering the portrait position. No identity swap or garment morph. New room/camera geography makes the collection change intelligible without narration.

## Chapter 2 — Signature / Our Own Image

Objective: people help make one another's images, then include the photographer as a subject. Diverse adult cast. Oakland context comes from real location/reference research and maker authorship; no invented documentary history. Daylight studio/courtyard with a chair, camera and reflector, not mandatory bridge scenery.

S1 — “Signature takes me back to where it all started.” Establish the photographer and subject on opposite sides of the camera. Hold the subject's complete Signature look before camera movement.
S2 — “Oakland is part of that. It’s part of me.” Wider geography with grounded place details supplied by actual references. Do not invent a founder's childhood home, atelier or archival photo.
S3 — “And when you put these pieces on, I want you to bring your own story with you.” Subject moves the chair into a preferred position. Photographer watches and adjusts; choice visibly affects the picture.
S4 — “The way you wear it. The way you carry yourself.” A second subject chooses a different stance while a helper changes reflector angle. Hold garment fronts without hands/gear covering them. No choreography forcing personalities to match.
S5 — “I made the piece… but I want to see what you do with it.” Subject offers to take the camera. Photographer transfers it securely, hands separate, then walks into the already established portrait space. Never generate a fused camera/hand interaction and accept it as close enough.
S6 — Pause/payoff: former photographer settles into their own posture; new photographer frames them. End on the person being seen, not a synchronized group smile. No generated camera display needed.

Transition S→L: camera lowers to reveal the photographer's face; cut to a similarly framed Love Hurts protagonist looking partly away. Hold the new bench geography long enough to establish that this is a different story, not a continuation of the same person's wardrobe.

## Chapter 3 — Love Hurts / The Place Beside Me

Objective: the guarded protagonist chooses whether to permit closeness. Diverse adult cast, two people. Bench with a free place occupied by the protagonist's bag; quiet courtyard/conservatory. No literal beast, scars, castle, rescue or new relationship biography. The protagonist owns the decision.

L1 — “With Love Hurts, I kept thinking about the Beast’s side of the story.” Medium view establishes person, bag, blocked seat and entrance in one coherent geography.
L2 — “Wanting somebody close… and being scared to let them get that close.” Reverse/over-shoulder eyeline as the second person arrives and pauses at a respectful distance. They do not demand the seat or touch the bag.
L3 — “You can care about somebody and still take a little time to let them in.” Return to the protagonist's hand on the strap and their face. Stay on their decision rather than external pressure. Product detail remains source-bound and legible.
L4 — “Sometimes it starts small.” Protagonist looks from the arriving person toward the blocked seat. Establish the intention before motion.
L5 — “You move your bag. You make a little room beside you.” Protagonist lifts bag to their own lap, then toward their outside hip; keep the adjacent seat visibly clear. Exact Fannie artwork must remain correct and unoccluded in a deliberate detail hold.
L6 — “And you let them sit.” The other adult sits, leaving some space. Do not add a kiss, sudden embrace or forced smile.
L7 — Final silent hold: hand on strap relaxes; protagonist turns slightly toward the other person. End without another voiceover sentence, invented tagline or automatic sales CTA.
