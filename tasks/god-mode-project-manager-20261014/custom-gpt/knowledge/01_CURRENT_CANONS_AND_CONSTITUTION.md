# SkyyRose Brand Constitution v1

**Owner:** Corey Foster · **Ratified:** 2026-09-23

This is a canonical record of the supplied owner decisions, not runtime

## Permanent identity and durable expression

### A — L1

SkyyRose is authentically rooted in its Oakland origin, founder authorship and
lived history. Future work must not fictionalize, erase, contradict or replace
that origin.

### D.origin — L1

SkyyRose preserves its authentic founder-and-family origin.

### D.legacy — L2

Legacy may inform storytelling without requiring family imagery in every
campaign.

### B — L1

Every major SkyyRose collection must have authentic authored meaning grounded in
the brand's lived identity, perspective, values, history, or evolving story.
Autobiography is a major source of that meaning but is not the only permissible
source. A collection must not be reduced to an arbitrary aesthetic, trend, or
visual concept without an authentic underlying reason for its existence.

### C — L1

SkyyRose is fundamentally gender-neutral in brand positioning and presentation
and is not defined by conventional men's/women's categories. Individual
products, people, styling, campaigns, or stories may use gender-specific context
when intentionally appropriate, but that context must not silently redefine the
brand or canonical product identity.

### G1 — L2

SkyyRose favors specific, authored, context-rich expression over generic luxury
conventions. Minimal, restrained, or abstract executions are valid, but they
should remain deliberate and meaningfully connected to the collection, product,
brand, place, story, or applicable creative context rather than becoming
interchangeable with another luxury label.

### G2 — L2

Major SkyyRose collections should have a coherent authored narrative context
rooted in their underlying meaning, but individual executions do not need to
tell the entire story. When narrative devices are used—story, atmosphere,
symbolism, place, character, sequence, or environmental interaction—they should
serve the collection, product, or meaning rather than exist as disconnected
spectacle.

### G3 — L2

SkyyRose may express collection identity through environments, spatial
storytelling, materials, light, place, symbols, character, or other
world-building devices when they meaningfully support the collection. The
durable principle is intentional, authored contextualization—not a requirement
for literal rooms, 3D scenes, monuments, bridges, thrones, or any particular
environment.

### G4 — L2

SkyyRose may separate storytelling from transactional presentation, but the two
should remain meaningfully connected. Editorial, cinematic, spatial, symbolic,
or environmental experiences should provide an understandable route toward the
relevant collection or products when used in a commerce context. Product
identity, price, availability, selection, and purchase state remain factual
commerce concerns and must not be obscured, fictionalized, or subordinated to
storytelling.

### G5 — L2

SkyyRose treats atmosphere, wonder, emotional impact, and spectacle as
legitimate creative purposes. Immersive techniques—including animation, 3D,
spatial interaction, cinematic transitions, environmental storytelling, and
emerging media—do not need to function solely as transactional utilities.
However, experiences introducing substantial complexity, interaction cost,
performance cost, or technical overhead should earn that complexity through an
intentional role in storytelling, emotional impact, product discovery,
exploration, brand experience, or another defined purpose. Technical
sophistication alone is insufficient justification.

### G6 — L2

SkyyRose may use mystery, exploration, unconventional navigation, delayed
revelation, or intentional creative friction when those choices meaningfully
serve the experience. Creative work does not need to behave like conventional
ecommerce. However, users must not become unintentionally trapped, lose
essential orientation, or lose access to critical product and commerce actions.
Essential controls and appropriate accessibility, preference, device, and
capability fallbacks should remain understandable and recoverable.

### G7 — L2

SkyyRose uses cinematic thinking as a compositional, narrative, and experiential
discipline rather than as a mandatory visual effect. Cinematic expression may
emerge through framing, sequencing, pacing, scale, tension, reveal, atmosphere,
sound, silence, movement, stillness, place, character, product focus,
interaction, or spatial experience. No particular grading, darkness, camera
movement, film grain, transition style, aspect ratio, 3D technique, or visual
effect is inherently required. SkyyRose should actively explore new forms of
fashion shopping, product discovery, storytelling, and interaction that give
customers experiences beyond conventional ecommerce patterns. Innovation should
create meaningful discovery, emotion, participation, understanding, surprise, or
connection between customer, collection, story and product rather than novelty
for novelty's sake. 3D rooms, cinematic scrolling, environmental discovery,
interactive worlds, spatial commerce, game-like exploration, adaptive
storytelling and emerging technologies are creative territories—not permanent
templates. Future work should be free to invent materially different shopping
experiences when they preserve product truth, brand identity, recoverable
control, accessibility and commerce integrity. A still, bright, minimal,
physical, conversational, spatial, playful, documentary, photographic, or
yet-uninvented experience may be as authentically SkyyRose as today's animated
cinematic environments when it carries intentional direction, authored meaning
and compelling customer experience.

### G8 — L2

SkyyRose is expected to evolve creatively rather than repeatedly reproduce
previously successful executions. Major campaigns, collection experiences, and
significant creative initiatives should introduce materially new creative
territory through one or more dimensions such as visual direction, materials,
environments, storytelling mechanisms, interaction models, media, technology,
composition, product discovery, or shopping experience. Innovation may combine
multiple dimensions when the result is coherent. Existing motifs, technologies,
materials, environments, or interaction patterns may be reused when contextually
meaningful, but previous success alone is insufficient justification for
repetition. SkyyRose should not optimize itself into sameness. Historical
repetition and short-term performance are evidence, not permanent creative
boundaries. Performance evidence may inform future decisions but may not
automatically redefine Brand DNA, eliminate meaningful experimentation, or
permanently narrow creative territory.

- character-led journeys;
- mini-games;
- collectible systems;
- augmented physical/digital experiences;
- spatial product discovery;
- conversational or adaptive experiences;
- future interaction models not yet defined.

No one mechanism becomes permanent merely because it succeeds.

## K4-001 — Kids Product Fidelity

> **Creative freedom around Kids never reduces product fidelity requirements. Any actual SkyyRose Kids Capsule product represented visually must remain an exact, verifiable representation of the real product.**

The Kids world may be fantastical.

The garment may not be fictionalized.

## K5-001 — Character and World Separation

Recommended additional rule:

> **Characters, mascots, rooms, environments, and narrative objects belong to contextual creative expression unless separately ratified. Their existence does not grant authority to redesign products, logos, collection marks, origin history, or other protected truth.**

This prevents a powerful character/world system from slowly becoming the authority for the whole brand.

# P — Palette Authority

The archaeology identified a real color-authority conflict: historic “no blue” wording does not accurately describe all current environment, interface, natural-location, and staging color behavior. Product color, interface color, environmental color, and protected brand assets therefore need separate authorities.

Ratify the following.

## P1-001 — Color Authority Is Domain-Specific

> **SkyyRose does not use one universal color authority for every visual domain. Product color, protected brand/collection asset color, interface color, environmental color, photographic color, campaign palette, lighting color, and natural-location color are separate scopes.**

Never infer permission across those scopes automatically.

---

## P2-001 — Product Color Is Locked

> **Actual SkyyRose product colorways must reproduce the authoritative real product exactly. Campaign palette, lighting, environmental art direction, interface colors, or atmospheric effects may not silently recolor the product.**

Example:

```text
Blue sky                 allowed
Blue environmental light context-dependent
Blue interface status    allowed when appropriate

Actual black garment
rendered as blue          NOT allowed
```

The Exact Product Fidelity Gate remains controlling.

---

## P3-001 — Protected Marks and Artwork

> **Approved logos, collection marks, artwork, garment graphics, and protected assets retain their governed color and material treatments where applicable. Environmental palette freedom does not automatically authorize recoloring protected marks.**

Asset permissions remain scoped by actual approval.

---

## P4-001 — Environmental and Campaign Palette Freedom

> **Environmental, editorial, cinematic, and campaign palettes may explore broadly—including palettes outside historically dominant dark, black, metallic, crimson, gold, silver, or rose-gold treatments—when the palette meaningfully supports the collection, concept, location, material system, narrative, channel, or experience.**

This is important for creative evolution.

The Creative OS should not default to:

> black + chrome + dramatic red/gold

simply because those treatments dominate recent work.

---

## P5-001 — Natural Color Is Not Brand Recoloring

> **Truthful natural colors appearing through skies, water, vegetation, architecture, streets, people, materials, documentary photography, Bay Area locations, or other real-world environments do not automatically establish or violate a master-brand palette rule.**

This directly protects the Bay Area discovery principle.

A blue-gray sky does not mean:

> blue is now a master SkyyRose brand color.

Likewise, a historical ban cannot automatically erase truthful natural color from a location.

---

## P6-001 — Accessible Interface Color

> **Interface colors may adapt as necessary for accessibility, contrast, semantic state, readability, interaction feedback, and usability without redefining product color or master-brand identity.**

UI red, blue, green, warning yellow, focus rings, and similar semantic colors should not accidentally become apparel or campaign authority.

---

## P7-001 — Palette as Innovation Dimension

> **Palette may itself be a deliberate dimension of creative evolution. Major campaigns should not be forced into historically dominant dark or metallic treatments solely for recognition.**

The system should be able to explore:

- high-key light;
- daylight;
- natural color;
- muted documentary palettes;
- vivid color;
- monochrome;
- unexpected material palettes;
- neutral architectural tones;
- seasonal palettes;
- Bay Area environmental color;
- collection-specific systems;
- future directions not currently represented.

Always subject to protected truth.

---

## P8-001 — Palette Provenance

Recommended addition:

> **When a palette is materially consequential to a campaign or collection, record whether its authority comes from product truth, protected brand assets, collection context, campaign art direction, environment, accessibility requirements, or creative proposal.**

This makes future conflicts much easier to resolve.

# S — Saturation, Novelty, Experimentation & Learning

The archaeology already identified repeated use of dark surfaces, monuments, cathedral imagery, thrones, metallic marks, bridge/night compositions, chapter language, procession structures, and other creative devices. It explicitly warns that repetition can build recognition but that similarity must not become brand authority.

Ratify the following.

## S1-001 — Creative Saturation Awareness

> **Before major creative production, review recent and relevant SkyyRose work for repeated visual devices, materials, environments, locations, compositions, motifs, technologies, narrative structures, camera languages, interactions, shopping mechanics, and campaign patterns.**

This is awareness, not prohibition.

The purpose is to know when repetition is intentional versus automatic.

---

## S2-001 — Material Novelty Requirement

> **Major campaigns, collection experiences, commercials, short films, significant web experiences, and other major creative initiatives should introduce materially new creative territory through one or more meaningful dimensions.**

Novelty dimensions may include:

```text
visual direction
material language
palette
environment
location
architecture
composition
camera language
story structure
character
sound
media
motion
technology
interaction
product discovery
shopping mechanic
physical/digital relationship
channel behavior
```

Multiple dimensions may be combined.

---

## S3-001 — Difference Must Be Meaningful

> **Novelty cannot be satisfied by cosmetic variation alone. The creative system must be able to state what is materially different from recent work and why that difference matters to the intended customer, collection, narrative, shopping experience, or campaign objective.**

Examples that do **not** automatically qualify:

- chrome → brushed chrome;
- dark room → slightly different dark room;
- same monument → different camera angle;
- same campaign structure → different text;
- same shopping mechanic → different animation.

---

## S4-001 — Creative A/B/n Hypotheses

> **Major creative initiatives should explore multiple materially distinct hypotheses before expensive final production when practical.**

Prefer A/B/n over token variation.

Each direction should describe:

- hypothesis;
- underlying meaning;
- visual direction;
- material language;
- environment/location;
- narrative approach;
- product role;
- interaction/shopping idea;
- novelty relative to recent work;
- execution approach;
- risk;
- expected learning.

---

## S5-001 — Experiment Classes

Maintain four distinct experiment types.

### Creative Direction

Question:

> Which conceptual direction or world best expresses the intended meaning?

### Experience

Question:

> Which interaction, navigation, product-discovery, or shopping model creates the strongest customer experience?

### Execution

Question:

> Which composition, copy, edit, format, hook, sequence, framing, or production execution works best?

### Market

Question:

> Which released version best produces the intended customer or business outcome?

Do not collapse these into one generic “A/B test.”

---

## S6-001 — Synthesis Is Allowed

> **Experimentation is not winner-takes-all. Strong elements from multiple directions may be deliberately combined when their synthesis produces a coherent concept stronger than the individual candidates.**

Example:

```text

strongest material system

B
strongest narrative

C
strongest shopping mechanic

        ↓

D
A materials
+
B narrative
+
C interaction

        ↓

new prototype
```

The synthesis must itself be tested.

Do not feature-stack unrelated ideas merely to appear innovative.

---

## S7-001 — Performance Does Not Rewrite Brand DNA

> **Advertising, engagement, conversion, commerce, audience, or creative performance may inform future execution, channel strategy, and hypothesis selection, but performance alone may not automatically redefine Brand Constitution, product truth, or permanent creative boundaries.**

A high-performing generic execution means:

> it performed well in that tested context.

It does not mean:

> generic execution is now SkyyRose identity.

---

## S8-001 — Never Optimize Into Sameness

> **SkyyRose must not optimize itself into creative sameness. Historical success, recent creative similarity, and short-term performance are evidence rather than permanent creative limits. Future work should continue testing materially new territory.**

The system should optimize:

```text
RECOGNIZABLE IDENTITY
        +
MEANINGFUL NOVELTY
        +
CUSTOMER EXPERIENCE
        +
PRODUCT TRUTH
```

not simply visual similarity to previous campaigns.

---

## S9-001 — Novelty and Brand Compliance Are Independent Axes

Recommended hard rule:

> **A creative direction should be evaluated separately for constitutional compliance and meaningful novelty. High similarity to prior SkyyRose work is not proof of brand correctness, and high novelty is not proof of brand violation.**

Conceptually:

```text
                NOVELTY
                  ↑
                  │
       innovative │ innovative
       but wrong  │ + on-brand
                  │
─────────────── BRAND COMPLIANCE ───────→
                  │
       repetitive │ familiar
       + wrong    │ + on-brand
                  │
```

Desired major creative work generally aims toward:

> **on-brand + materially new**
