# Research report: storyboarding and pre-production for fashion campaign films, AI-assisted pipelines, short-form structure rules, and AI-disclosure law

Method: 12 exa searches (3 per sub-question, each with a recency-weighted variant), 6 deep reads (web_fetch_exa, 6,000 chars), 96 URLs in the ledger. All claims are [docs]-scope (pages fetched this session); nothing is [live] or [repro]. Research date: 2026-09-12.

## Findings

### Q1. Documented pre-production workflow and what storyboard/shot-list templates contain

- The documented pipeline for a fashion campaign film is brief → mood board (plus optional "sonic board") → treatment → shot list → storyboard → previz/animatic → shoot → edit → cutdowns. A 2026 production checklist lists it as: define brief (concept, audience, platform, deliverables), build mood board, scout locations, cast, prep wardrobe by look and shooting order, write shot list covering every setup/angle/camera move, confirm crew and call times. [Snappyit](https://snappyit.ai/blog/fashion-video-workflow) 2026-06-03. A parallel four-week fashion-film playbook puts pre-pro in week 1 (concept doc with story/sequence, releases, music decision, mood board + reference deck), shoot week 2, post week 3, and adds a "sonic board" of audio references alongside the visual mood board. [Vidpros](https://vidpros.com/fashion-film/) 2026-07-23. (Sonic board: SINGLE-SOURCE.)
- A 10-week campaign timeline puts creative development (concept, moodboard, visual system) at weeks 7–6, pre-production (casting, location, styling, samples, crew) at weeks 6–4, "production readiness" (final shot list, call sheet, product check, backups signed off) at week 3, and insists the shot list is not the first content document: an asset map by channel/placement comes first. [Phil Halfmann, Fashion Campaign Timeline](https://philhalfmann.com/fashion-campaign-timeline-how-to-plan-every-stage-from-strategy-to-launch/) 2026-07-22. Author's opinion: "a moodboard alone is not a production plan."
- Shot lists should be built around content categories (hero, product-focused, social vertical, movement, close crops for ads, clean e-com frames), not outfits alone; label every shot essential/supporting/optional and assign product, model, styling, location, orientation, intended channel, plus setup time. [Phil Halfmann, Plan a Fashion Campaign Shoot](https://philhalfmann.com/how-to-plan-a-fashion-campaign-shoot-that-delivers-better-content-and-better-results/) 2026-04-16 and the timeline article above.
- Lookbook shot-list template fields: one row per look or SKU combination with columns for model, garment IDs, accessories, background, mandatory frames, optional frames, crop requirements, props, completion status; standard per-look frame menu = straight-on full length, three-quarter full length, back/rear three-quarter, mid-length styling, fabric/construction/accessory detail, movement frame, horizontal with text space, vertical for Stories/Reels. [Signal Studio NYC](https://signalstudionyc.com/blog/fashion-lookbook-shot-list) 2026-08-20.
- Generic shot-list schema (industry standard, multiple sources agree): shot #, scene #, shot size (EWS…ECU, OTS, insert), camera angle, lens, movement (static/pan/tilt/dolly/track/handheld/gimbal/drone/zoom), description, audio, notes (lighting, wardrobe continuity flags, VFX). [GetTemplated](https://gettemplated.com/shot-list-template/) n.d.; an 18-column variant adds framing, sound, VFX, setup time. [M Studio](https://mstudio.ai/features/shot-list-template) n.d.
- Storyboard panel fields: frame artwork, scene/frame number (e.g. "SC 2 · FR 5"), action/description, dialogue/VO, shot size, camera movement, duration in seconds (running total checks the board fits a :30), sound/notes (SFX, music, supers). Frame format should match the deliverable (16:9, 9:16, 1:1, 2.39:1). [VoiceDeck](https://voicedeck.io/tools/storyboard-creator/) n.d. StudioBinder's commercial template toggles scene number, description, audio and video fields per panel and recommends 3–6 panels per page; its five essentials are descriptions, dialogue, images/sketches, directional arrows, shot references. [StudioBinder template](https://www.studiobinder.com/templates/storyboards/commercial-storyboard-template/) 2023-09-20; [StudioBinder guide](https://www.studiobinder.com/guides/storyboards/how-to-create-a-commercial-storyboard/) 2025-12-18.
- Commercial "frame card" format with more per-frame data than a film board: duration (must total :15/:30/:60), visual description, camera movement, VO words, supers (logo, legal, CTA), SFX, music notes. A :30 typically needs 8–12 frames; board the four non-negotiables first (opening hook, product hero, benefit demo, CTA frame); average modern commercial shot is 2–3 s, so 10–15 shots per :30; get written client sign-off, then build a "production board" adding lens/f-stop/frame rate, lighting, wardrobe, VFX per setup. [StoryBirdie](https://storybirdie.com/blog/how-to-storyboard-a-commercial) 2026-03-28. (Frame-card spec: SINGLE-SOURCE, though consistent with VoiceDeck.)
- Treatment structure for commercials: intro/why-you, product highlight, casting, story of the spot (length, emotions), gear/look, budget (sometimes), outro; 5–12 pages. [No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/commercial-treatment) 2024-02-07. A 2026 director's method adds: director's statement, concept/narrative, visual world with curated reference frames (captioned with intent), tone and pacing, casting/wardrobe/production design, cinematography approach, music/sound, why-you; delivered as an image-heavy PDF. [Storyflow](https://storyflow.so/blog/how-to-write-a-directors-treatment) 2026-07-22. Director Miranda May (LBB) says her treatments refine the brief to a log line, define themes/mood/emotion/audience, and that she now uses "visual AI in all my treatments." [LBBOnline](https://lbbonline.com/news/From-Fashion-Floors-to-Virtual-Stages-Miranda-Mays-Vision-for-the-Next-Wave-of-Advertising) 2025-11-24.
- Lookbook vs campaign film: the campaign film is 60–90 s "concept-driven filmmaking" where garments are "costume design of a short film"; the lookbook is "systems over inspiration" (locked lighting plot, repeatable camera move, a shot menu per look: full-length, three-quarter, detail, movement). Schedule around "wardrobe math": a full look change with hair takes ~25 min vs 15 min to relight. The DP should frame key setups for both 16:9 and vertical extraction. [Decade Pictures](https://www.decadepictures.com/fashion-video-production-nyc/) 2026-07-24.
- Practitioner case studies: Mangos Miami shoot used a Pinterest mood board and three five-frame shot lists (framing, pose family, camera angle, orientation, lighting) with individual → two-model → group sequencing. [Phil Halfmann case study](https://philhalfmann.com/mangos-miami-fashion-campaign-pre-production/) 2026-02-08. La Maison Simons "Dress Yourself": editor and director did extended pre-vis for tone; music house pulled hundreds of reference tracks; edit driven by movement, track locked after days of editing. [LBBOnline](https://lbbonline.com/news/This-Canadian-Fashion-Chain-Is-Celebrating-Clothes-that-Truly-Spark-Joy) 2025-10-24. Art director Jahved Crockett: shoot motion and stills in the same sequence, one creative bridge across photo/video, keep "every look accurate — fabric, prints, skin tones." [Daily Front Row](https://fashionweekdaily.com/inside-the-vision-of-art-director-jahved-crockett-behind-victorias-secret-pink-x-loveshackfancy-and-alice-olivia-fall-24-campaigns/) 2025-10-18. Alyosha's Posié film: static camera for frozen poses, frenetic camera for rave; shot all static frames first, then motion. [Directors Notes](https://directorsnotes.com/2025/10/22/alyosha-rave-or-freeze/) 2025-10-22.
- Multi-output planning: "One shoot, ten outputs" fails when cutdowns/BTS/stills are bolted on late; design the day around formats. [NOT Studio](https://www.not.studio/monitor/we-will-hold-the-camera-and-the-chaos) 2025-06-23 (opinion piece). Saint + Sofia's in-house process: concept → moodboards → pre-production (locations, schedules, crew, casting, shot lists) → styling → shoot → grade/retouch/sequence. [Saint + Sofia](https://euro.saintandsofia.com/blogs/backstage/the-art-of-creating-a-fashion-campaign) 2026-06-02.

### Q2. Generative-AI storyboarding/previz/campaign video workflows and pitfalls (2025–2026)

- Agency six-stage AI pre-pro pipeline (each with a human owner): 1 brief synthesis (LLM), 2 insight mining, 3 concept + moodboards (40–80 reference frames per territory → 3 routes), 4 AI storyboards + 5–10 s animatic clips per key beat (Runway/Kling/Veo), 5 style-reference locking (character sheets, LoRAs) "the step most teams skip and later regret", 6 shot list + previz handoff to producer. Worked example: 30-s social spot, brief to approved boards in 48 h (12 storyboard panels, 6-s clips per beat). Pitfall named: "the plausible but wrong storyboard" that is unshootable; producer review non-negotiable. [Absolutely AI](https://www.absolutelyai.com.au/news/ai-pre-production-campaigns) 2026-08-09. (Deep-read.)
- Stills-first is the consensus fashion workflow: produce and approve stills (casting, garment, world, grade), choose frames to move, animate from them with motion described separately. A 30-s piece = 15–20 shots of 1–3 s across beats Open (1–2), Introduce (2–3), Detail (3–4), Motion (2–3), Looks (4–6), Close (1–2). What still breaks: walking gait, fabric under sustained motion, hands touching garments, small-detail continuity across cuts, faces at length. Continuity "is a setup property, not an edit property": "Describing 'the same woman in the red coat' in fifteen prompts produces fifteen women in fifteen red coats." [Astria](https://www.astria.ai/articles/ai-fashion-film-guide/) 2026-08-20. (Deep-read.) Corroborated by "approve the stills, not the clips" and cost logic (stills ~3% of render cost). [DesignerBox](https://designerbox.ai/blog/ai-fashion-video/) 2026-08-06.
- Character/garment consistency technique stack (multiple sources agree): (a) build a reference set/character sheet of 2–7 images (front, three-quarter, side, full body, wardrobe close-up, prop close-up), neutral lighting, plus a word-for-word written description reused verbatim; (b) register it as a tagged reference (Runway Gen-4 `@ref`, Kling Elements, Veo Ingredients, Pika Scene Ingredients); (c) chain clips last-frame → first-frame; (d) do not paraphrase prompts; (e) do not mix tools or model versions mid-project; (f) seed-locking is only a tiebreaker. [AI Tools Guidebook](https://aitoolsguidebook.com/en/articles/ai-video-cut-continuity-broken/) 2026-05-21; [Pixo](https://pixo.video/blog/ai-character-consistency) 2026-07-07; [Flick Kling guide](https://flick.art/blog/kling-character-consistency) n.d.; [VidAU](https://www.vidau.ai/ai-characters-how-to-get-consistent-results-every-time/) 2026-03-04; [Igly](https://igly.ai/blog/ai-character-consistency-video-reusable-reference-workflow-2026) 2026-08-15. Note Veo 3.1 cannot combine a first frame with reference images in one generation (AI Tools Guidebook, SINGLE-SOURCE).
- Product-sheet discipline for garments/products: five reference angles per garment (fabric close-up, front, side, back, worn) plus a hand-scale reference; cast faces before costumes; write a "texture language" paragraph per material; three-PDF briefing (agent brief, lookbook, shooting script with one fabric-movement note per shot); validate at close/mid/wide before scaling; clip rejection ran 75–85%. [invideo fashion/product ads](https://invideo.io/blog/ai-fashion-product-ads/) 2026-07-15 (vendor content, SINGLE-SOURCE on rejection rate).
- Runway Gen-4 fashion case study: half-day photo shoot optimized for AI input (extra space around product, even lighting, poses that "could plausibly continue into movement"), Motion Brush for fabric, 8 generations of 4–5 s assembled into a 30-s hero, single LUT across all clips. Reported failures: hands, warped garment labels/tags (fixed with post overlays), model identity drift "despite identical prompts" (masked by quick cuts and grading). [inyourleague](https://tools.inyourleague.net/en/runway-gen4-case-study-fashion-brand-seasonal-campaign-ai-video-en/) 2026-03-27 (SINGLE-SOURCE, unnamed brand).
- Garment-fidelity QC method: list must-preserve features per garment (lapel shape, button count, pocket placement, hem length, print scale/direction) as rejection criteria; test one motion brief across three source cases (clean studio, backlit outdoor, layered outfit); review frame-by-frame at turns/overlaps; score "correct or changed" before style; use labels like print drift, closure change, fused layers, edge melt, invented accessory; "the source record wins over the generated frame." [Pure Magazine](https://puremagazine.co.uk/fashion-motion-fails-when-garment-details-start-drifting/) 2026-08-04.
- Prompt-level rules for garment clips: give motion a timed structure ("4-second reveal, then hold 2 seconds"), name fabric physics, lock the camera (24fps, 85mm equivalent, no flicker); keep clips 6–8 s and cut together. Four manual checkpoints: garment truth vs real product, hands/edges, brand judgment, disclosure decision. [Vantaige](https://vantaige.io/blog/ai-fashion-campaign-images-video-2026) 2026-07-22.
- "Montage not campaign" pitfall: statistically independent generations produce sets lit "from five different directions" that read as a montage, not a collection; the fix is system-level identity inputs, not longer prompts; run a 5–10 image coherence test before generating 40. [Rainfrog](https://www.rainfrog.ai/blog/why-ai-image-generation-fails-for-campaigns) 2026-06-10 (vendor content).
- Storyboard-to-video tutorial pattern: one style descriptor repeated in every prompt; generate a reference still, then the first frame of each scene as a still, then animate; one camera move per clip (whip pans and rack focus confuse generators); cull artifacts immediately; single grade pass to unify tools. [Promptyze](https://promptyze.com/storyboard-to-video-with-ai-how-to-go-from-rough-sketch-to-polished-clip-using-kling-runway-and-friends/) 2026-02-26.
- Lookbook pipeline: brand-model character reference generated first (6–8 variants, saved as FINAL), fixed prompt structure [character lock][garment][pose][environment][lighting][shot type/aspect], per-garment hero 4:5 / three-quarter 4:5 / detail 1:1 / lifestyle; image-to-video via Kling for model shots and Veo for environmental B-roll; 10-minute LUT pass. [Cliprise](https://www.cliprise.app/learn/workflows/ecommerce/fashion-brand-lookbooks-ai-video-image-pipeline) 2026-03-17.
- Midjourney V7 role: strongest for moodboards, lookbook drafts, runway previz and 5–10 s micro-films around the hero shoot, not replacing it; `--cref`/`--sref`/`--seed` for series; V7's `--oref` (Omni Reference, weight 0–1000) replaced `--cref`. [SurePrompts](https://sureprompts.com/blog/midjourney-v7-for-fashion-editorial) 2026-04-08; [Creative AI News](https://www.creativeainews.com/articles/ai-character-consistency-workflow-2026/) 2026-06-19. Trained-identity options (Soul ID, Flux.2 fine-tune from 15–30 images, IC-LoRA in ComfyUI for camera/body) hold better on high shot counts than single-image references. [Higgsfield](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/tools-for-consistent-ai-characters) n.d. (vendor). ComfyUI still path: SDXL + IPAdapter FaceID Plus v2 at 0.7–0.8 weight + ControlNet OpenPose + FaceDetailer; stills only. [Flick ComfyUI](https://flick.art/blog/img2img-consistent-character/comfyui) 2026-06-26.
- Practitioner opinion on scope: AI should be used for storyboards, mood boards and scripts, with human craft for capture and assembly [Vidpros]; synthetic models "with no real-world counterpart" still backfire in fashion; use AI for variants/supporting content, and model releases from 2024 or earlier usually lack AI-likeness rights. [Versely](https://www.versely.studio/blog/ai-video-for-fashion-brands-lookbooks-2026) n.d. (vendor).

### Q3. Structural rules for short-form fashion ads and launch teasers

- Official Google: Shorts ads can be up to 3 min but only the first 60 s play in the Shorts feed; recommend <60 s and 9:16; action-oriented ads 10–30 s; the CTA button appears at 3 s for Performance Max/App/Demand Gen and at 10 s for Video View/Reach campaigns; provide a 40-char headline and 90-char description for the CTA overlay card. [Google Ads Help, Shorts specs](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16041697?hl=en) n.d. Vertical 9:16 "deliver better performance compared to landscape assets"; the player can compress to 1:1 when the user scrolls, cropping top and bottom. [Google Ads Help, square/vertical](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9128498) n.d.
- Official Meta: 9:16 recommended for Stories/Reels; 4:5 for Facebook Feed single image, 1:1 for Instagram Feed. [Meta aspect ratios](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/103816146375741) n.d. Safe Zone: for 9:16 ads in Stories, Reels, Feed and in-stream reels keep top, bottom and sides free of key elements; if a Reels ad carries disclaimers leave the bottom 40% free; taller-than-9:16 screens may zoom and crop outside the safe zone; ads "usually only have one call to action." [Meta safe zone](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/980593475366490) n.d. (Deep-read.) Instagram Stories plays video <16 s in full; ≥16 s may be split into up to 3 cards. [Meta Stories ad specs](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/update/video/instagram-story) n.d. Meta reports vertical sound-on Reels creative delivered 4.8% lower CPA, 5.1% higher CTR, 2.9% higher conversion vs other video (Meta's own study). [Meta Reels updates](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/reels-ads-updates-performance-features-automated-creative-suitability-solutions) n.d.
- Official TikTok Creative Codes: hook/body/close structure; "90% of ad recall impact is captured within the first six seconds"; product on screen drives +65% brand affinity, +25% recall; CTA cards +45% recall, +19% likeability; faster scene changes draw viewers in early; 88% of users say sound is vital; shoot vertical and respect the UI "safe space." [TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads) 2024-09-25. (Deep-read.)
- Third-party safe-zone pixel figures (not official): Reels top ~14% / bottom ~35% (250 px / 670 px on 1080×1920), Stories top ~14% / bottom ~20% (250 px / 340 px). [rule1](https://rule1.ai/articles/instagram-ad-sizes) 2026-03-06; [Clarigital](https://www.clarigital.com/codex/paid-advertising/facebook-instagram/stories-reels-ads/) 2026-04-05. One source claims Meta consolidated Stories/Reels safe zones in March 2026 (top 14%, bottom 35%). [hy.digital](https://hy.digital/blog/meta-ad-formats-2026) 2026-07-23 (SINGLE-SOURCE, cites Superscale).
- Hook timing (industry consensus, non-official): decision window is 1.5–3 s; Reels effectively ~2 s; open on motion or the garment, never a logo; save brand ID for the last 3–5 s. [Adlibrary Meta video guide](https://adlibrary.com/posts/meta-video-ads-guide-2026) 2026-05-17; [AdRiseLab](https://adriselab.com/blog/vertical-video-meta-ads-9-16-production-specs) 2026-04-21; [Tellos](https://www.jointellos.com/blog/ai-video-ads-fashion-tiktok-reels-content-at-scale) 2026-03-31. Audio hook in first 0.5 s on Reels; hook rate ≥35–45% strong, <20% failing. [Adlibrary Reels](https://adlibrary.com/posts/reels-ads) 2026-05-10.
- Length by objective (third-party syntheses, roughly consistent): Meta cold 15–30 s, awareness 6–15 s, Stories 7–15 s per card; TikTok sweet spot 15–30 s with a 9-s floor before reach is capped (operator claim) and 21–34 s cited as TikTok's own completion-optimal range. [Ad-Stack](https://www.ad-stack.ai/blog/tiktok-ads-specs-and-benchmarks-2026/) 2026-05-21; [ConversionStudio](https://conversion.studio/blog/video-ad-best-practices) 2026-07-17; [Benly](https://benly.ai/learn/ad-creative/meta-ads-creative-specs-2026) 2026-03-25. Fashion-specific: 6–10 s for a new-collection launch (fast cuts, bold overlays), 9–15 s for traffic, 21–34 s for conversions; 12-s example = hook 1–3 s (model walking to camera), 4–8 s two close-ups (fabric, neckline), 9–12 s wide + "Shop the Collection." [PicJam](https://www.picjam.ai/blog/how-long-should-a-tiktok-ad-be) 2026-02-11.
- Beat structures: four-part Hook (0–3 s) / Problem (3–8 s) / Solution-demo (8–20 s) / CTA (last 3–5 s). [ConversionStudio]. Six-beat Reels arc Hook, Setup, Tension, Value, Payoff, CTA with discovery timing Hook 1–3 s, Body 5–11 s, CTA 1–2 s; fashion transformation template 10–20 s; CTA placed 1–3 s from the end, one ask only. [Storyfiers](https://storyfiers.com/latest-content/how-to-structure-a-story-for-instagram-reels/) 2026-06-03. Creator-brief three-layer build: hook 0–3 s, narrative core 3–25 s, conversion signal final 5 s. [Influencers Time](https://www.influencers-time.com/instagram-and-tiktok-creator-briefs-for-fashion-brands/) 2026-06-06.
- Shot counts: 15–30 s with 3–5 hard cuts (hook, problem, reveal, demo, CTA). [Ad-Stack]. Drop teasers: 5–8 s with "48 hours" text and a reminder CTA; 12–16 clips from a 2-hour batch shoot. [CopyCut fashion](https://www.copycut.app/guides/tiktok-marketing-for-fashion) 2025-10-27. Launch calendar: pre-launch days −21 to −7 at 1–2 posts/day, launch week 2–3/day, 30–60 videos over 3–4 weeks; full reveal held for day −3 to 0. [CopyCut launches](https://www.copycut.app/guides/tiktok-strategy-for-product-launches) 2025-10-27.
- Production rule: build the 9:16 master first, then 4:5 feed and 1:1; re-cut pacing and text per placement rather than re-uploading one file; burned-in captions (one vendor claims 87% of fashion Reels are muted, SINGLE-SOURCE [Versely]). [ConversionStudio]; [Benly]; [TikAdSuite](https://tikadsuite.com/blog/tiktok-ads-for-fashion-brands/) 2025-12-18.

### Q4. Disclosure, ethics and legal considerations for AI fashion imagery

- EU AI Act Article 50 transparency obligations apply since 2 August 2026; providers must machine-readably mark synthetic outputs (50(2)); deployers (brands/agencies) must disclose deepfakes (50(4)) "in a clear and distinguishable manner" at first exposure (50(5)); the Commission adopted guidelines 20 July 2026 and a voluntary Code of Practice with EU icons ("AI GENERATED", "AI MODIFIED"). [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content) 2026-07-31; [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/08/EU-AI-Act-Transparency-Obligations-for-AI-Generated-Content-Article-50) 2026-08 (deep-read). Orrick: for video the label must appear at the beginning, after interruptions and at regular intervals; end credits alone do not comply; icons are optional but labelling is not.
- The Digital Omnibus (Reg. (EU) 2026/1744) deferred high-risk obligations but not Article 50; the only relief is a provider-side grace to 2 December 2026 for systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026; deployer duties are live now. [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2026/08/eu-ai-acts-transparency-rules-what-went-into-effect-on-2-august) 2026-08-12; [DesignerBox labeling](https://designerbox.ai/blog/labeling-ai-generated-fashion-images/) 2026-08-06.
- Scope for fashion: the trigger is a realistic synthetic person/object/scene, not "AI use." Commission guidelines list colour correction, background extension/replacement for aesthetic purposes, product arrangement and rescaling as outside the duty; a photorealistic AI model wearing your garment is inside; an AI product image that makes the product look better than reality is a deepfake and a label is the wrong fix. Deployers "cannot rely on the machine-readable marking" alone; disclosure must be visible. Commercial content rarely qualifies for the artistic-work reduced disclosure. [DesignerBox labeling]; [DG Law](https://www.dglaw.com/eu-ai-act-guidance-expands-ai-disclosure-rules-for-advertisers-and-pr-teams/) 2026-07-31. Non-EU brands are caught if the ad is displayed in the EU.
- Penalties: up to €15M or 3% of worldwide turnover (whichever higher); for SMEs and startups whichever is lower (Art. 99(6)). [DesignerBox labeling]; [Billo](https://billo.app/blog/eu-ai-act/) 2026-08-10.
- US: no federal "AI-generated" label rule for product images; FTC Section 5 covers deception about material facts, AI endorsements/testimonials, and native ads; max civil penalty cited $53,088 per violation. [Dynamis LLP](https://www.dynamisllp.com/knowledge/ai-disclosure-laws-are-coming-what-brands-need-to-know-now) 2025-10-27; [MODA AI](https://modaai.app/blog/ai-model-disclosure-shopify-fashion) 2026-06-21. New York's Synthetic Performer Disclosure Law (GBL §396-b amendment) effective 9 June 2026 requires conspicuous disclosure of any AI-generated human performer in ads reaching NY audiences; $1,000 first / $5,000 subsequent violation; exemptions for expressive works, audio-only, translation. [Prizmad](https://prizmad.com/guides/ai-ad-disclosure-labels) 2026-07-31; [MODA AI]. NY Fashion Workers Act (effective 2025) requires written consent before creating/using a model's digital replica; Tennessee ELVIS Act and California AB 1836 cover unauthorized replicas. [Fashion Law Journal](https://fashionlawjournal.com/ai-models-vogue/) 2025-08-04.
- Platform labels: Meta auto-applies "AI info" via C2PA/IPTC detection and its own tools; self-disclosure mandatory only for social-issue/election/political ads (stricter since 1 June 2026). TikTok requires advertisers to manually enable an AI Disclosure tag in Ads Manager (visible "Contains AI-generated content" label); undisclosed content later detected faces rejection and account review; TikTok Shop requires matching labels on ad and listing; TikTok auto-labels C2PA content and joined the C2PA steering committee 28 July 2026. Google added a "How this ad was made" panel; third-party AI content must be self-flagged in Google Ads. [Prizmad]; [Billo]; [AuditSocials](https://www.auditsocials.com/blog/eu-ai-act-article-50-advertising-compliance-2026-synthetic-content-labeling-marketer-obligations-enforcement) 2026-04-20 (AuditSocials asserts brief flash or end-card-only disclosures fail the prominence standard; treat as legal-vendor interpretation).
- Case history: Guess's August 2025 Vogue US print ad used AI models by Seraphinne Vallora disclosed in six words of small print; a TikTok flagging it drew 2M+ views; Vogue's position was that it was paid placement, not editorial; Condé Nast confirmed no AI model has appeared editorially in Vogue. [Nine/CNN](https://www.nine.com.au/lifestyle/ai-generated-models-guess-vogue-backlash-20250801-p5qqvz.html) 2025-07-31; [WearView](https://www.wearview.co/blog/brands-using-ai-fashion-models) 2026-06-26. Seraphinne Vallora photographed a real model for a week to inform poses (CNN interview via Nine).
- H&M digital twins: 30 real models, built with Uncut, models retain rights to their twins and are paid per classic image-use terms, output carries prominent watermarks; first campaign July 2025; drew less heat than Guess.
