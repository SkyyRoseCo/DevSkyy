## Executive Summary

The three workflows share one spine: **lock a stable narrative and a locked visual reference system first, produce stills before motion, and treat the launch as a gated migration with a T-minus calendar rather than a date.** Brand-story practice has converged on a short "narrative spine" that stays fixed for years while channel layers change per collection, and on a generic-story test: if the story could be pasted onto a competitor, it is not finished ([Corrado Manenti](https://www.corradomanenti.it/en/creating-a-brand-story-for-fashion-and-luxury-brands/); [Fitinline](https://fitinline.com/article/read/storytelling-mistakes-that-make-fashion-brands-feel-generic/)). Storyboarding for fashion film follows brief, mood board, treatment, shot list, board, previz, shoot, edit, and the AI-assisted version of that pipeline is stills-first with a locked reference set, because continuity "is a setup property, not an edit property" ([Astria](https://www.astria.ai/articles/ai-fashion-film-guide/); [Absolutely AI](https://www.absolutelyai.com.au/news/ai-pre-production-campaigns)). Collection introduction runs on an eight-to-twelve-week calendar with a formal go/no-go gate, a three-to-four-week pre-drop for drop-model brands, and an email sequence whose later "proof" and "deadline" sends often outperform the launch send itself ([Phil Halfmann](https://philhalfmann.com/fashion-product-launch-checklist/); [Landing Partners](https://www.landing.partners/blog/fashion-drop-marketing-collection-launch-strategy); [Talk Shop](https://www.letstalkshop.com/blog/shopify-product-launch-email-sequence)). Since 2 August 2026, EU AI Act Article 50 makes visible disclosure of realistic synthetic people or scenes a legal duty for anyone whose ad is shown in the EU, and New York's synthetic-performer law is in force since June 2026 ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content); [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/08/EU-AI-Act-Transparency-Obligations-for-AI-Generated-Content-Article-50); [Prizmad](https://prizmad.com/guides/ai-ad-disclosure-labels)).

## 1. Brand-story development workflow

### 1.1 The frameworks practitioners actually use

- **Narrative spine plus narrative layers.** One sentence naming hero, ordinary world, transformation, and special world, built on the four pillars people, places, purpose, plot. Writing order: open on a specific moment, name the protagonist and their precise problem, describe the ordinary world, identify the turn, show the special world. The spine stays fixed for years; layers (founder video, origin editorial, product narrative templates, campaign briefs) change per collection ([Corrado Manenti](https://www.corradomanenti.it/en/creating-a-brand-story-for-fashion-and-luxury-brands/), 2026-06-08). Single-source for the "spine" term.
- **Five luxury narrative types**: heritage arc, aspiration mirror, cultural stance, creator mythology, transformation narrative. The author's recommendation for a young premium brand is the aspiration mirror, with the stated risk of feeling aspirational without depth ([Corrado Manenti](https://www.corradomanenti.it/en/great-brand-stories-lessons-from-luxury-fashion-icons/), 2026-05-05). Author opinion.
- **Belief before product.** Bob Sheard's four-part engine (world, authority, personality, purpose) is positioned as "not a brand book"; his critique is that most brands "cobble together nice language after the fact" ([1 Granary](https://1granary.substack.com/p/the-brand-whisperer-bob-sheard-and), 2025-09-28). Single-source.
- **Brand Universe Framework** sequences belief, identity, cultural positioning, "truest fan" (defined by worldview and media diet, not demographics), then lifestyle, creative universe, icons and rituals, cultural stance, voice, legacy ([Strategy Dept.](https://strategydept.substack.com/p/the-brand-universe-framework), 2025-04-29). Paywalled after stage four; single-source.
- **Archetype workshop to narrative map to channel routing** is documented luxury practice ([Fashion Strategy Weekly](https://www.fashionstrategyweekly.com/p/fsw-insights-supra-referentiality-8c3), 2024-04-08), with two online experiments finding archetype-based stories lift perceived quality and emotional brand value ([Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/43339232/Communicating_Luxury_Brands_through_Stories), 2020).
- **Streetwear-specific guideline structure**: foundation (purpose, values, subcultures, personality, voice, founder story) before visuals, then a fixed Core (logo geometry, wordmark, philosophy, primary palette, core type, voice) versus a Flexible layer (campaign graphics, collection art, photo style, seasonal colour, collab identity), plus a drop framework naming sacred versus negotiable elements ([Confetti Design](https://confetti.design/blog/streetwear-brand-guidelines)).
- **World-building as the streetwear alternative to a linear arc**: recurring characters, naming systems, packaging and release mechanics; the test is "whether the clothing still works when the story is removed" ([Multiversity](https://www.multiversitystore.com/blogs/multiversity-journal/beyond-logos-streetwear-that-tells-a-story-through-design), 2025-08-30). Kith is described as an ecosystem of touchpoints rather than a mark ([Culture Codes](https://culturecodes.substack.com/p/61-building-worlds-not-just-brands), 2025-09-08).
- **Customer-as-hero, brand-as-guide** is the dominant SEO-tier framework (origin, hero, belief, before/after transformation, assembled story; 80 to 150 words for an About page) ([Tech Pack Genius](https://techpackgenius.com/how-to-create-a-strong-brand-story-for-your-clothing-line/), 2025-11-27). No source cited StoryBrand or the Golden Circle by name.

### 1.2 A real "brand book first" operating model

Burberry's 2024-2026 reset, as relayed from a Vogue Business interview with its CMO: step one a brand book codifying founder story, values, iconic products, symbols, logo usage and tone; step two a design, merchandising and marketing "triad" with social embedded on set; step three KPIs in three buckets (brand commerce, brand buzz, brand love); step four four customer archetypes ([ALM Corp](https://almcorp.com/blog/burberry-trench-coat-campaign-luxury-marketing-social-media/), 2026-03-19). Single-source, secondhand. The March 2026 "Portraits of an Icon" campaign used the trench as organizing device, 23 portraits by Tim Walker, a documentary film, and social Q&A planned on set ([ALM Corp](https://almcorp.com/blog/burberry-trench-coat-campaign-luxury-marketing-social-media/)).

### 1.3 How streetwear labels embed the story in launch content (2024-2026)

- **Fear of God Essentials Fall 2025**: founder-led, era-specific brand film. Jerry Lorenzo DM'd Hype Williams; the short recreates a scene from *Belly* and pulls "the emotions and the feeling" of the era, not the aesthetic ([Complex](https://www.complex.com/style/a/dimassanfiorenzo/hype-williams-jerry-lorenzo-fear-of-god-essentials-short-film-belly), 2025-08-26; [Fear of God](https://fearofgod.com/pages/fear-of-god-presents-a-film-by-hype-williams)). No outcome metrics reported. A separate vendor case study claims the rebuilt "shoppable brand world" site produced the brand's best sales week and +150% recall ([CaseStudies.com / Kettle](https://www.casestudies.com/company/kettle/case-study/fear-of-god-boosts-brand-recall-150-with-kettle)). Single-source, vendor-reported.
- **Kith x Giorgio Armani (Sept 2024)**: pitched with a physical book, then a founder thank-you post, a three-piece Monday Program drop, then the full collection four days later, with four short films for four customer archetypes ([Triple J Studios](https://triplejstudios.com/en-us/blog/kith-giorgio-armani), 2024-09-17). Single-source.
- **Corteiz**: story enacted offline, city by city. A 2025 four-city US tour used a decommissioned subway station in New York and Atlanta's Magic City; the founder frames it as "exporting a London energy to the world" ([The Star, NYT syndication](https://www.thestar.com.my/lifestyle/style/2026/01/18/the-rise-of-a-streetwear-cult-brand-built-on-scarcity-spectacle-and-grit), 2026-01-18). Revenue of roughly $58M for 2024 is attributed to the NYT by two independent secondary sources ([Complex](https://www.complex.com/style/a/lei-takanashi/corteiz-streetwear-everything-to-know), 2026-01-15; [Collater.al](https://www.collater.al/en/corteiz-brand-history-streetwear/), 2026-08-20).
- **Aimé Leon Dore**: "scaled the aspiration before scaling the inventory," with lookbooks and editorial establishing the world before wide assortment, and an SS25 documentary-style campaign film ([Fashionography](https://thefashionography.com/men/collections/the-world-of-aime-leon-dore-nostalgia-exclusivity-and-the-new-luxury/), 2025-09-10; [Rare Moments](https://www.raremoments.media/fashion/aim-leon-dore-shares-creative-documentary-style-fashion-film-to-showcase-spring-summer-2025-campaign), 2025-02-22).
- **Oaklandish** is the closest civic-pride analogue: it began as a guerrilla public-art project projecting Oakland history onto buildings, apparel came second, shirts were deliberately "for those in the know and not tourists," and at least 10% of revenue goes to local groups ([SFGate](https://www.sfgate.com/bayarea/article/Oaklandish-opens-T-shirt-shop-to-rebrand-city-2335578.php), 2011; [SF Chronicle](https://www.sfchronicle.com/style/article/Oaklandish-Booming-business-rooted-in-Oakland-6176294.php), 2015; [East Bay Express](https://eastbayexpress.com/gifts-from-oaklandish-give-back-1/), 2020). Its 2025 #MakeItBay collab frames the Bay as "a heartbeat," not a backdrop ([Oaklandish](https://www.oaklandish.com/blogs/news/makeitbay-x-oaklandish-wear-the-future-of-bay-area-storytelling), 2025-07-03). Coverage of its narrative is dated; no 2024-2026 press analysis was found.
- **Gucci "The Tiger" (SS26)** introduced six archetypes in a lookbook first, then silence, then a film that replaced the runway, on an owned shoppable hub with minimal paid ([Golden Hour Co.](https://www.goldenhourcodigital.com/blog/masterclass-gucci-the-tiger-how-to-turn-storytelling-into-strategy), 2025-10-05). Single-source; critics later called the metaphor disconnected from heritage ([Fashion Strategy Weekly](https://www.fashionstrategyweekly.com/p/substance-and-spectacle-a-structured), 2026-02-07).
- **Format roles** in an agency's 2026 model: tentpole campaign creates desire; the brand film is "the cousin that gets to be strange" with a long reputational payoff; the lookbook is the workhorse and source of e-commerce imagery; behind-the-scenes is proof; creators are reach. Streetwear "runs on drops, community, and speed" and "will punish anything that feels corporate" ([Raised Media](https://www.raisedmediaco.com/research/fashion-brand-content)).
- **Founder POV** is described by one vendor as the best-performing single format for new brands, produced as one half-day shoot cut into roughly 40 pieces over an eight-week drop cycle ([Vidpros](https://vidpros.com/fashion-marketing/), 2026-07-16). Single-source, vendor.

### 1.4 Failure modes and how to test a story

- **The transferability test.** "A fashion brand feels generic when its story could be transferred to a competing company without materially changing the meaning." Root causes: mood board first then reverse-engineered story; stopping at category vocabulary ("culture, independence, limited access"); founder story with no specific underserved occasion; claims without inspectable product evidence. The nine-step repair ends with a memory test at 24 to 48 hours: what does this brand believe, which product detail represents it, which statement do you trust least, what would you expect it to launch next ([Fitinline](https://fitinline.com/article/read/storytelling-mistakes-that-make-fashion-brands-feel-generic/), 2026-07-21).
- **Retell test and proof rule.** Can a team member who did not write it retell it in two minutes; is every purpose claim backed by at least two concrete decisions ([Corrado Manenti](https://www.corradomanenti.it/en/creating-a-brand-story-for-fashion-and-luxury-brands/)).
- **Heritage is a liability when misused.** Peer-reviewed work found heritage-focused storytelling reduced ad evaluation and purchase intent for close-to-self products ([Journal of Advertising](https://doi.org/10.1080/00218499.2026.2648909), 2026-04-22; abstract only). EU courts now treat historical references as commercial claims judged on overall impression; "inspired by our archives" is not "from our archives" ([Luxe Daily](https://www.luxe-daily.fr/en/luxury-heritage-storytelling-cjue/), 2026-04-03). Single-source each. "Heritage is the most misused word in luxury marketing" and "scarcity is not a story" ([Y Breakfast Studio](https://ybreakfaststudio.com/2026/02/18/luxury-brand-marketing/), 2026-02-18). Opinion.
- **Storyproving over storytelling**: demonstrate craft rather than claim it ([Luxus Plus](https://luxus-plus.com/en/column-storytelling-is-dead-long-live-storyproving/), 2025-12-15).
- **AI-generic copy and imagery.** Audiences accept AI in functional product shots and reject it in campaign and editorial, where authorship lives; J.Crew x Vans was called "slop" and the Guess ad "just wrong" ([Vogue](https://www.vogue.com/article/how-can-fashion-brands-get-ai-campaigns-right), 2025-10-09; [The Interline](https://www.theinterline.com/2025/09/09/efficiency-vs-ethicals-in-consumer-facing-ai-campaigns/), 2025-09-09). AI trained on past comms reproduces tone but "replication is not synonymous with a consistent exercise of taste" ([The Interline](https://www.theinterline.com/2025/08/27/authorship-in-the-age-of-automation/), 2025-08-27). Logo-off test for voice: collect 20 to 30 pieces, strip the logo, ask whether people still know the brand ([Advertising Week](https://advertisingweek.com/aw360/news/when-it-comes-to-content-the-choice-isnt-human-vs-ai-its-generic-vs-genuine/9514), 2025-10-21).
- **Testing protocols with decision rules** (B2B-derived, portable): Stranger Test (five ICP-matched strangers, 30 seconds on the hero, four of five must say what, who, and which problem), Clone Test (strip logos from yours and three competitors, four of five pick yours and cite the differentiator), Champion Test (unaided retell), run in 48 hours ([Bare Strategy](https://barestrategy.com/blog/b2b-messaging-validation-framework), 2026-05-22). Test the message as plain text before creative on comprehension, relevance, differentiation, credibility, intent; six to ten interviews then a panel ([The Marketing Juice](https://themarketingjuice.com/message-testing-market-research/), 2026-04-08). Sandbox in email or paid social before touching the homepage or manifesto; recall means customers or press repeat your phrasing ([Vestd](https://www.vestd.com/blog/how-to-test-your-market-story-before-you-commit), 2025-07-30). Story KPIs: recall at 24 to 48 hours, association strength, engagement duration ([Ulsh Agency](https://theulshagency.com/2025/06/05/how-to-measure-refine-your-brand-story-for-maximum-impact/), 2025-06-05).

## 2. Storyboard and pre-production workflow

### 2.1 The conventional pipeline

Brief, mood board (optionally a "sonic board" of audio references), treatment, shot list, storyboard, previz or animatic, shoot, edit, cutdowns ([Snappyit](https://snappyit.ai/blog/fashion-video-workflow), 2026-06-03; [Vidpros](https://vidpros.com/fashion-film/), 2026-07-23). A ten-week campaign timeline places creative development at weeks seven to six, pre-production at six to four, and "production readiness" (final shot list, call sheet, product check, backups signed off) at week three, and insists an asset map by channel comes before the shot list: "the shot list should not be the first content document" ([Phil Halfmann](https://philhalfmann.com/fashion-campaign-timeline-how-to-plan-every-stage-from-strategy-to-launch/), 2026-07-22).

**Treatment** structure: intro, product highlight, casting, story of the spot, look, sometimes budget, outro, five to twelve pages ([No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/commercial-treatment), 2024-02-07); a 2026 version adds a director's statement, captioned reference frames, tone and pacing, and sound, delivered as an image-heavy PDF ([Storyflow](https://storyflow.so/blog/how-to-write-a-directors-treatment), 2026-07-22).

**Lookbook versus campaign film**: the film is 60 to 90 seconds of concept-driven filmmaking where garments are costume; the lookbook is "systems over inspiration," with a locked lighting plot and a repeatable shot menu per look, and the day is scheduled around "wardrobe math" (a full look change with hair takes about 25 minutes versus 15 to relight) ([Decade Pictures](https://www.decadepictures.com/fashion-video-production-nyc/), 2026-07-24). Shoot motion and stills in one sequence with one creative bridge and "every look accurate, fabric, prints, skin tones" ([Daily Front Row](https://fashionweekdaily.com/inside-the-vision-of-art-director-jahved-crockett-behind-victorias-secret-pink-x-loveshackfancy-and-alice-olivia-fall-24-campaigns/), 2025-10-18). Design the day around formats, or cutdowns and BTS get bolted on late ([NOT Studio](https://www.not.studio/monitor/we-will-hold-the-camera-and-the-chaos), 2025-06-23).

### 2.3 The AI-assisted pipeline (2025-2026)

- **Six-stage agency blueprint**, each stage with a human owner: brief synthesis, insight mining, concept and mood boards (40 to 80 reference frames per territory, narrowed to three routes), AI storyboards plus five-to-ten-second animatic clips per beat, style-reference locking (character sheets, LoRAs), then shot list and previz handoff. Locking references is "the step most teams skip and later regret." Named pitfall: the plausible but unshootable storyboard; producer review is non-negotiable ([Absolutely AI](https://www.absolutelyai.com.au/news/ai-pre-production-campaigns), 2026-08-09).
- **Stills-first is the consensus.** Produce and approve stills (casting, garment, world, grade), choose which frames move, animate from them. A 30-second piece is 15 to 20 shots of one to three seconds. What still breaks: walking gait, fabric under sustained motion, hands touching garments, small-detail continuity across cuts, faces held at length. "Describing 'the same woman in the red coat' in fifteen prompts produces fifteen women in fifteen red coats" ([Astria](https://www.astria.ai/articles/ai-fashion-film-guide/), 2026-08-20). "Approve the stills, not the clips"; stills cost roughly 3% of render spend ([DesignerBox](https://designerbox.ai/blog/ai-fashion-video/), 2026-08-06).
- **Consistency stack** (multiple sources agree): a reference set of two to seven images (front, three-quarter, side, full body, wardrobe and prop close-ups) in neutral light plus a verbatim written description; register it as a tagged reference in the tool; chain clips last frame to first frame; never paraphrase prompts; never mix tools or model versions mid-project; seed-locking is only a tiebreaker ([AI Tools Guidebook](https://aitoolsguidebook.com/en/articles/ai-video-cut-continuity-broken/), 2026-05-21; [Pixo](https://pixo.video/blog/ai-character-consistency), 2026-07-07; [Igly](https://igly.ai/blog/ai-character-consistency-video-reusable-reference-workflow-2026), 2026-08-15). Trained identities (LoRA fine-tunes from 15 to 30 images) hold better at high shot counts than single-image references ([Higgsfield](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/tools-for-consistent-ai-characters)). Vendor.
- **Garment-fidelity QC**: list must-preserve features per garment (lapel shape, button count, pocket placement, hem length, print scale and direction) as rejection criteria; review frame by frame at turns and overlaps; score "correct or changed" before style; "the source record wins over the generated frame" ([Pure Magazine](https://puremagazine.co.uk/fashion-motion-fails-when-garment-details-start-drifting/), 2026-08-04). Five reference angles per garment plus a hand-scale reference; one vendor reports 75 to 85% clip rejection ([invideo](https://invideo.io/blog/ai-fashion-product-ads/), 2026-07-15). Single-source on the rate.
- **Prompt rules for garment clips**: timed motion ("4-second reveal, then hold 2 seconds"), named fabric physics, locked camera, six-to-eight-second clips cut together; four manual checkpoints: garment truth versus real product, hands and edges, brand judgment, disclosure decision ([Vantaige](https://vantaige.io/blog/ai-fashion-campaign-images-video-2026), 2026-07-22).
- **"Montage, not campaign"**: independent generations lit from five directions read as a montage; the fix is system-level identity inputs, not longer prompts; run a five-to-ten image coherence test before generating forty ([Rainfrog](https://www.rainfrog.ai/blog/why-ai-image-generation-fails-for-campaigns), 2026-06-10).
- **Reported failures in a Runway Gen-4 seasonal campaign**: hands, warped garment labels (fixed with post overlays), and model identity drift "despite identical prompts," masked with quick cuts and a single LUT across clips ([inyourleague](https://tools.inyourleague.net/en/runway-gen4-case-study-fashion-brand-seasonal-campaign-ai-video-en/), 2026-03-27). Single-source, unnamed brand.
- **Scope opinion**: use AI for storyboards, mood boards, scripts and supporting variants; keep human capture for the hero; fully synthetic models still backfire in fashion; pre-2024 model releases usually lack AI-likeness rights ([Versely](https://www.versely.studio/blog/ai-video-for-fashion-brands-lookbooks-2026)).

### 2.4 Short-form structure rules

Official platform guidance:

- **Google/YouTube Shorts**: only the first 60 seconds play in the Shorts feed; action ads 10 to 30 seconds; the CTA button appears at 3 seconds for Performance Max, App and Demand Gen, and at 10 seconds for Video View and Reach; 9:16 outperforms landscape and the player may crop to 1:1 on scroll ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16041697?hl=en); [Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9128498)).
- **Meta**: 9:16 for Stories and Reels, 4:5 for Facebook feed, 1:1 for Instagram feed; keep top, bottom and sides of 9:16 clear; with disclaimers leave the bottom 40% free; ads "usually only have one call to action"; Stories video under 16 seconds plays whole, longer may split into up to three cards ([Meta safe zone](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/980593475366490); [Meta aspect ratios](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/103816146375741); [Meta Stories specs](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/update/video/instagram-story)).
- **TikTok Creative Codes**: hook, body, close; "90% of ad recall impact is captured within the first six seconds"; product on screen +65% affinity; CTA cards +45% recall; respect the UI safe space ([TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads), 2024-09-25).

Third-party consensus (not official): decision window 1.5 to 3 seconds; open on motion or the garment, never a logo; brand ID in the last 3 to 5 seconds ([Adlibrary](https://adlibrary.com/posts/meta-video-ads-guide-2026), 2026-05-17; [AdRiseLab](https://adriselab.com/blog/vertical-video-meta-ads-9-16-production-specs), 2026-04-21). Fashion-specific lengths: 6 to 10 seconds for a new-collection launch, 9 to 15 for traffic, 21 to 34 for conversion; a 12-second example runs hook 1-3s (model walking to camera), 4-8s two close-ups, 9-12s wide plus "Shop the Collection" ([PicJam](https://www.picjam.ai/blog/how-long-should-a-tiktok-ad-be), 2026-02-11). Six-beat Reels arc hook, setup, tension, value, payoff, CTA, with CTA placed one to three seconds from the end and one ask only ([Storyfiers](https://storyfiers.com/latest-content/how-to-structure-a-story-for-instagram-reels/), 2026-06-03). Drop teasers of 5 to 8 seconds; pre-launch days minus 21 to minus 7 at one to two posts a day, launch week two to three, full reveal held for day minus 3 to 0 ([CopyCut](https://www.copycut.app/guides/tiktok-strategy-for-product-launches), 2025-10-27). Build the 9:16 master first, then 4:5 and 1:1, re-cutting pacing and text per placement rather than re-uploading one file ([ConversionStudio](https://conversion.studio/blog/video-ad-best-practices), 2026-07-17; [Benly](https://benly.ai/learn/ad-creative/meta-ads-creative-specs-2026), 2026-03-25). Safe-zone figures are now first-party (corrected in round 2): Meta's Reels and Stories spec pages say to leave at least 14% of the top, 35% of the bottom and 6% of each side free ([Meta Ads Guide, Instagram Reels video](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/update/video/instagram-reels); [Meta Ads Guide, Instagram Stories image](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/update/image/instagram-story)). No official Stories-only 20% figure exists; that number was third-party.

### 3.2 The drop model

- **Three phases**: pre-drop weeks minus four to minus one; launch window 24 to 72 hours; post-drop from day three. Email sequence: week four story with no product photos, week three first teaser, week two behind-the-scenes, week one countdown with VIP early access of one to two hours. Meta: 30 to 40% of budget on awareness pre-drop, 60 to 70% on conversion in the first 48 hours, conversion campaigns off pre-drop. Launch-day gate: at least six to eight product images with one on model, real-time stock messaging, checkout tested on three phones, back-in-stock flow live, no launch-day discount. Post-drop: hold full price two to four weeks. "Almost always, the problem was a 3-day pre-drop instead of a 3-week pre-drop" ([Landing Partners](https://www.landing.partners/blog/fashion-drop-marketing-collection-launch-strategy)). Agency experience; the "3-5x first-48-hour revenue" claim is single-source.
- **Email sequencing**: teaser and waitlist T-10 to T-14, early access T-1 to T-2, launch T-0, social proof T+1 to T+3, last call T+5 to T+7; "Emails 4 and 5 typically recover more revenue than the launch email itself, because they catch people who needed proof or a deadline" ([Talk Shop](https://www.letstalkshop.com/blog/shopify-product-launch-email-sequence), 2026-06-25). Klaviyo's own three-stage matrix reserves SMS for exclusive or time-sensitive sends ([Klaviyo Academy](https://academy.klaviyo.com/en-us/quick-guides/promote-a-new-product-with-omnichannel-marketing)). Point drop campaigns at a dedicated landing page carrying story, lookbook and full lineup, not a filtered collection ([Fudge.ai](https://www.fudge.ai/guides/klaviyo-flows-fashion/), 2026-08-27). Fire email and SMS off one trigger in the same minute; independently scheduled sends drift five to twenty minutes and customers hit unpropagated pages ([US Tech Automations](https://ustechautomations.com/resources/blog/automate-dtc-product-launch-klaviyo-postscript-2026), 2026-06-18).
- **Corteiz operating facts**: password-gated site open only days at a time; "a bad drop is, like, five to 6,000 orders on a drop day... a good drop is anything upward of 10 to 12 to 13,000"; roughly 300 items at a time; no wholesale; no paid ads ([The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/lifestyle/style/2026/01/18/the-rise-of-a-streetwear-cult-brand-built-on-scarcity-spectacle-and-grit), 2026-01-18). Sunday 7pm London teasers with no image or price ([Finally Offline](https://www.finallyoffline.com/article/corteiz-sunday-7pm-teaser-rulestheworldcup-tour-m3k7p9wq), 2026-07-01; aggregator, treat as unverified). 1,000 customers lined up at an East Harlem school for a one-day Nike pop-up ([BoF](https://www.businessoffashion.com/articles/marketing-pr/gamified-fashion-drops-releases-corteiz-nike/), 2025-05-02).
- **Broken Planet Market**: Sunday 8pm UK single-hour windows, monthly calendar, teaser is one video and one image with no names or prices, product photography appears only when the site updates; an early-2026 disclosed AI-model campaign split its community and the brand returned to real photography ([Finally Offline](https://www.finallyoffline.com/article/broken-planet-market-drop-may-2026-scarcity-announcement-video-r5k2p8nx), 2026-05-15; [Finally Offline](https://www.finallyoffline.com/article/broken-planets-second-character-drops-sunday-mqu0zhhq), 2026-06-25). Aggregator with internal contradictions; single-source.
- **Scarcity has a ceiling.** Supreme's revenue fell from $561.5M to $523.1M against a $600M target, VF wrote down $735M and sold for $1.5B versus $2.1B paid; "scarcity and growth are really oppositional" ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/operations/scarcity-and-growth-are-oppositional-how-streetwear-legend-supreme-lost-its-luster/), 2024-08-06; [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/07/17/vf-dumps-supreme-shows-m-and-a-dangers-cool-brand/), 2024-07-17; [BoF](https://www.businessoffashion.com/briefings/retail/is-it-time-for-supreme-to-sell-out/), 2024-07-19). Vogue's "end of the hype machine" piece treats Corteiz's owned-distribution model as the counter-example ([Vogue](https://www.vogue.com/article/is-this-the-end-of-the-hype-machine), 2025-07-25).
- **Drop calendars**: Supreme Thursday 11am ET with the week previewed Monday or Tuesday; Palace Friday 11am ET with lookbook Thursday; Kith Monday Program 11am ET ([Restock Blog](https://restock.blog/blog/streetwear-drop-guide/), 2026-01-26; cop-guide source, single-source on queue details). Confirmed 2026 activity for Supreme, Palace and Essentials ([Hypebeast](https://hypebeast.com/2026/6/best-drops-june-week-4-supreme-palace-fear-of-god), 2026-06-24).

### 3.3 Website relaunch plus collection introduction at once

- **Live example (Sept 2026)**: AllSaints released Aaron Esh's debut AW26 collection alongside a rebuilt content-driven site combining campaign imagery, lookbooks, articles and profiles, framed as "a new chapter of our story where we live up to our history" ([FashionNetwork](https://in.fashionnetwork.com/news/Allsaints-starts-new-chapter-with-first-aaron-esh-collection,revamped-website,1862716.html), 2026-09-02).
- **Treat it as a migration.** "A relaunch is not a design project. It is a migration project with a design component" ([Zenku](https://zenku.studio/en/blog/website-relaunch-checklist-2026/), 2026-06-02). Baseline twelve months of analytics, full URL map with 301s, rollback available 24 to 72 hours, escalation on a 10% drop in indexed pages or a 404 surge, daily checks for a week then weekly for 90 days, judged by business outcomes not Lighthouse scores ([Refact](https://refact.co/insights/migration/website-relaunch-playbook), 2026-08-24). Go live outside peak, tell existing customers in advance, prepare password resets and order-history migration, keep the old environment as rollback ([Clickpuls](https://clickpuls.com/en-AT/knowledge/shop-relaunch-checklist), 2025-12-02). Start the announcement "a few weeks in advance" with on-site, social and email teasers ([Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/ca/blog/website-relaunch), 2024-12-30).
- **Pre-order state is a trust problem first.** Frahm's unclear pre-order messaging drove hesitation; the agency fixed PDP, cart and checkout wording before rebuilding, and behaviour took about 45 days to stabilise post-rebuild ([WIRO](https://www.wiro.agency/blog/how-wiro-helped-frahm-turn-pre-orders-into-a-scalable-growth-engine), 2026-08-05). Agency-reported results.
- **Imagery gate for pre-orders**: "real photos should carry the burden of proof"; red flags are garment shown only in styled scenes, no seam or texture close-ups, inconsistent fabric across images ([Gorgeautiful](https://www.gorgeautiful.com/ai-lookbook-images-vs-real-photos-fashion-preorders/), 2026-05-29). "As long as the imagery matches what arrives in the box" is the trust condition ([Rewarx](https://www.rewarx.com/blogs/4-minute-test-ai-product-photos-shoppers), 2026-06-15). Return rates around 19 to 20% with expectation gaps a top driver ([Style3D](https://www.style3d.com/blog/truth-in-advertising-for-ai-product-renders-for-fashion-teams/), 2026-06-24). Vendor-authored.

### 3.4 Lookbook production and its place in the sequence

## 4. AI disclosure and imagery-truth constraints

- **EU AI Act Article 50** applies since 2 August 2026. Providers must machine-readably mark synthetic outputs; deployers (brands and agencies) must disclose realistic synthetic people, objects or scenes "in a clear and distinguishable manner" at first exposure; Commission guidelines of 20 July 2026 and a voluntary Code of Practice supply "AI GENERATED" and "AI MODIFIED" icons; for video the label must appear at the start, after interruptions and at intervals, and end credits alone do not comply ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content), 2026-07-31; [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/08/EU-AI-Act-Transparency-Obligations-for-AI-Generated-Content-Article-50), 2026-08). The Digital Omnibus deferred high-risk rules but not Article 50 ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2026/08/eu-ai-acts-transparency-rules-what-went-into-effect-on-2-august), 2026-08-12). Colour correction, background extension for aesthetics, product arrangement and rescaling are outside the duty; a photorealistic AI model wearing your garment is inside; an AI product image that flatters the product beyond reality is a deepfake where a label is the wrong fix ([DesignerBox](https://designerbox.ai/blog/labeling-ai-generated-fashion-images/), 2026-08-06; [DG Law](https://www.dglaw.com/eu-ai-act-guidance-expands-ai-disclosure-rules-for-advertisers-and-pr-teams/), 2026-07-31). Non-EU brands are caught if the ad is shown in the EU. Penalties up to €15M or 3% of turnover, lower of the two for SMEs ([Billo](https://billo.app/blog/eu-ai-act/), 2026-08-10).
- **United States**: no federal label rule for product images; FTC Section 5 covers deception about material facts and AI endorsements ([Dynamis LLP](https://www.dynamisllp.com/knowledge/ai-disclosure-laws-are-coming-what-brands-need-to-know-now), 2025-10-27). New York's Synthetic Performer Disclosure Law, effective 9 June 2026, requires conspicuous disclosure of any AI-generated human performer in ads reaching New York audiences ([Prizmad](https://prizmad.com/guides/ai-ad-disclosure-labels), 2026-07-31; [MODA AI](https://modaai.app/blog/ai-model-disclosure-shopify-fashion), 2026-06-21). The NY Fashion Workers Act requires written consent before creating or using a model's digital replica ([Fashion Law Journal](https://fashionlawjournal.com/ai-models-vogue/), 2025-08-04).
- **Platforms**: Meta auto-applies "AI info" via C2PA detection; TikTok's official ads API describes a self-disclosure toggle for non-Spark ads that adds an "Advertiser labeled as AI-generated" label in the search-results view, and applies its own "AI-generated" label to Spark Ads it detects as fully or significantly AI-made; the "mandatory tag" framing in round 1 came from a third-party page and is not on the official doc (corrected in round 2) ([TikTok API for Business](https://business-api.tiktok.com/portal/docs?id=1799186631031809)). TikTok Shop, by contrast, does require disclosure of fully or significantly AI content and bans AI that replaces or fabricates the product display ([TikTok Shop Academy US](https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=491489038501663&lang=en)). Google's "How this ad was made" panel is official, with visible overlays for campaigns targeting the EU, India, and New York ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17140115)).
- **Case history**: Guess's August 2025 Vogue print ad with AI models disclosed in six words of small print drew a two-million-view backlash; Condé Nast confirmed no AI model has appeared editorially ([Nine/CNN](https://www.nine.com.au/lifestyle/ai-generated-models-guess-vogue-backlash-20250801-p5qqvz.html), 2025-07-31). H&M's digital twins of 30 real models, who retain rights and are paid per image use, with prominent watermarks, drew less heat ([WearView](https://www.wearview.co/blog/brands-using-ai-fashion-models), 2026-06-26).
- **Convergent practitioner recommendations**: label adjacent to the image, not in a footer; specific wording ("AI model. Real garment.") beats "AI-enhanced"; written consent covering replica creation, media, territory, duration and pay before any real face; C2PA provenance plus a visible label; disclosure sign-off inside campaign approvals; the photographed reference is the source of truth in any dispute ([Dynamis LLP](https://www.dynamisllp.com/knowledge/ai-disclosure-laws-are-coming-what-brands-need-to-know-now); [MODA AI](https://modaai.app/blog/ai-model-disclosure-shopify-fashion); [Versely](https://www.versely.studio/blog/ai-video-for-fashion-brands-lookbooks-2026)).

### 6.2 AI labels and provenance, first-party

- **Meta** auto-applies an "AI info" label when its own generative tools create or significantly edit an asset, and when it detects third-party generative AI via C2PA metadata; advertisers can self-disclose in the European Region, California, New York, India, and Taiwan, where the label then sits next to the Sponsored mark; resizing and colour correction do not trigger it; Background Generation is available only for Advantage+ catalog ads ([Meta Business Help, AI info](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1010479435004531); [Meta Help Center](https://www.meta.com/help/artificial-intelligence/355108217670024/); [Meta Business Help, enhancements](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/297506218282224)). Every Advantage+ enhancement can be switched off per ad, some only in Advanced preview ([Meta Business Help, opt-out](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1082295769403815)).
- **Google** launched an Ads AI label setting in July 2026: designate assets as AI-created or edited, and campaigns targeting the EU, India, or New York get visible overlays; Google may label on its own where legally required and such labels cannot be overwritten ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17140115); [Merchant Center Help](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/17231950)). Merchant Center requires the IPTC `TrainedAlgorithmicMedia` tag on any AI-generated product image and forbids stripping it ([Merchant Center Help](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/14743464?hl=en)).
- **TikTok** ads: voluntary self-disclosure toggle for non-Spark ads, label shown in search-results view; auto "AI-generated" label on Spark content it detects; creators must label realistic AI content organically ([TikTok API for Business](https://business-api.tiktok.com/portal/docs?id=1799186631031809); [TikTok Support](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content)). TikTok Shop requires disclosure of fully or significantly AI content, allows background changes "as long as the product information is not altered," and bans AI that replaces the product display ([TikTok Shop Academy US](https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=491489038501663&lang=en)).
- **EU Article 50, from the Commission's own guidelines**: a deepfake requires content that "resembles existing persons, objects, places, entities or events and would falsely appear to a person to be authentic or truthful." Listed as not a deepfake: a real product against an AI-generated background if not misleading about the product; AI colour correction, background extension or replacement for aesthetic purposes, product arrangement, and rescaling. Listed as a deepfake: an AI product image that makes the product look better than reality, and a synthetic celebrity in an ad ([EC Guidelines PDF](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-07/guidelines_on_the_implementation_of_the_transparency_obligations_for_certain_ai_systems_under_article_50_of_the_ai_act_bzptwqhk0ikg1dtlddap41psfy_131215.pdf)). Icons: three official marks in black, white, and transparent variants, optional to use while labelling itself is mandatory; text alongside the icon tested better ([EC, EU icons](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/eu-icons-labelling-ai-generated-content)). Video cadence from the Code of Practice: icon at the start, at intervals where possible, and after every interruption ([Code of Practice PDF, mirror](https://noticias.ai/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/CoP_Transparency_of_AI_generated_content_jtHnEivPctLZtkoRFQrkbStffUY_129555.pdf)).
- **Attaching provenance**: `c2patool` signs a manifest with `digitalSourceType` set to `trainedAlgorithmicMedia` or `compositeWithTrainedAlgorithmicMedia`; the spec warns metadata is routinely stripped by non-C2PA platforms ([CAI local signing](https://opensource.contentauthenticity.org/docs/signing/local-signing/); [C2PA 2.4 guidance](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.4/guidance/Guidance.html)). YouTube carries forward C2PA 2.1+ disclosures; LinkedIn shows credentials on uploaded ad media ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15447836?hl=en); [LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a6282984)).
- **FTC**: the 2024 reviews rule bans fake AI testimonials but has no blanket ban on AI avatars; deception under the FTC Act still applies ([FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)). No FTC page on AI product imagery specifically was found.

### 6.6 WooCommerce launch operations, official

- **Pre-Orders** extension: charge upfront or upon release, optional availability date after which the product stops being a pre-order, "pre-ordered" order status, product-level only, fee and charge timing locked while active pre-orders exist, "disable automatic processing" for staging ([WooCommerce Pre-Orders docs](https://woocommerce.com/document/pre-orders/)).
- **Coming Soon mode** in core since 9.1: store-pages-only option, private share link, `Cache-Control: max-age=60` on the coming-soon page, and a warning that the live storefront "may not display immediately" because of server caching ([WooCommerce docs](https://woocommerce.com/document/configuring-woocommerce-settings/coming-soon-mode/); [WooCommerce dev blog](https://developer.woocommerce.com/2024/06/18/introducing-coming-soon-mode/)).
- **Back In Stock Notifications**: form appears after the out-of-stock status, queue runs in Action Scheduler batches, requires "Hide out of stock items" to be off ([WooCommerce docs](https://woocommerce.com/document/back-in-stock-notifications/)).
- **Hold stock**: the minutes value is the interval at which unpaid orders are cancelled, so cancellation lands between one and two intervals; a 10.1 regression stopped the job requeuing ([WooCommerce issue #24581](https://github.com/woocommerce/woocommerce/issues/24581); [WooCommerce issue #60375](https://github.com/woocommerce/woocommerce/issues/60375)).
- **Drop-day checklist** from an Automattic-owned host: staging load test, cache pre-warm, 12-hour change freeze, waiting room such as CrowdHandler or Queue-it, single-page checkout, cart fragments off on non-essential pages ([Pressable](https://pressable.com/blog/prepare-your-woocommerce-store-for-high-traffic-product-drop/); [Pressable KB](https://pressable.com/knowledgebase/woocommerce-high-traffic-event-preparation-guide-phase-2/)).

## Key Takeaways

- Write the one-sentence narrative spine and test it for transferability and 48-hour recall before any creative spend; the spine outlives every collection.
- Stills first, references locked, one grade: that is the whole AI pipeline. Continuity is decided at setup, not in the edit.
- A three-day tease is the documented failure mode; three weeks is the norm. Extend the organic tease backward without adding date claims.
- The proof send and the deadline send earn more than the launch send. Add both, product-state-safe.
- Real garment pixels carry the burden of proof; generated scenes need a disclosure decision recorded before activation.
- Treat the site relaunch as a migration with rollback and escalation triggers, not as a design launch.
- Real garment on an AI environment is, in the Commission's own example, not a deepfake; the label question is now a platform-mechanics question (Meta C2PA auto-label, Google designation, TikTok Shop rules), not a legal one.
- Flows convert at roughly eighteen times the rate of campaigns in the official Klaviyo data; make the launch's waitlist, early-access, and restock touches flows.
- Paid hooks lead with newness, story lives in owned channels; founder and behind-the-scenes formats out-hit the baseline on Meta.

## Methodology

Round 1 (2026-09-12): three parallel exa-backed research agents ran 34 searches (10 on brand story, 12 on storyboard and AI pipelines, 12 on launch workflows), each sub-question with at least two phrasings and one recency-weighted query, and 18 deep reads at up to 6,000 characters. Firecrawl was not available in this session. All 280 unique URLs returned by tool results are in the three agent ledgers saved alongside this report; every URL cited above appears in one of those ledgers. Claims supported by only one source are marked single-source inline. Round 2 (2026-09-13): three further agents ran 58 searches and 51 deep reads targeting first-party platform, regulator, and vendor pages, the five canonical reference brands and tier-one press, and named-dataset launch benchmarks; their ledgers are saved beside the round 1 ledgers. Two round 1 claims were corrected in place where first-party pages contradicted them. Sub-questions investigated: brand-story frameworks and deliverables; how streetwear and luxury labels embed story in launch content; story failure modes and testing; conventional pre-production and template contents; AI-assisted storyboard and video pipelines and their pitfalls; short-form structure rules from platforms; AI disclosure law; planned-launch calendars; the drop model; combined relaunch and collection introduction; lookbook production; launch KPIs and benchmarks.

## Gaps

Round 2 closed the Culture Kings and Palm Angels gap, replaced third-party platform figures with official ones, and added named datasets. Still open after round 2: the two most on-target Business of Fashion pieces are paywalled beyond their deks; no official per-second credit table for Kling 3.0 or price for GPT Image 2 exists on Higgsfield pages; TikTok's safe zone ships only as downloadable overlay files with no published pixel box; no platform publishes waitlist-to-purchase or first-week launch revenue for small apparel brands; Jerry Lorenzo's substantive storytelling quotes date from 2023 to 2024; the EC Code of Practice text was read from a mirror because the Commission-hosted file did not surface in search.

Round 1 gaps, for the record:

- Tier-one trade press was thin: Business of Fashion, Vogue and Complex surfaced for the drop model and two case studies; Vogue Business only secondhand; no Glossy, Hypebeast brand-story, WWD or Fashion United body text.
- Culture Kings and Palm Angels, two of the five canonical SkyyRose visual references, returned nothing on brand story or launch mechanics.
- Outcome metrics for launches are mostly vendor- or agency-reported and single-source. No verified sell-out times, unit counts or waitlist-to-purchase rates for small DTC fashion launches were found.
- No official Runway, Kling or Veo documentation was fetched; tool-capability claims come from third-party guides.
- No official Meta page gives numeric safe-zone pixels; figures cited are third-party.
- The Finally Offline aggregator contradicts itself on Broken Planet's founders; all of its figures are unverified.
- Two paywalled or truncated sources (Strategy Dept. stages five to ten; the Birau et al. paper) are cited from abstract or partial text only.
