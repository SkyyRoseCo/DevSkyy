This is a canonical record of the supplied owner decisions, not runtime implementation. The [verbatim handoff](OWNER-RATIFICATION-20260923.md), [machine-readable rules](constitution.json), and [decision history](owner-decisions.json) provide exact wording and provenance.

## Permanent identity and durable expression

### A — L1

SkyyRose is authentically rooted in its Oakland origin, founder authorship and lived history. Future work must not fictionalize, erase, contradict or replace that origin.

### D.origin — L1

SkyyRose preserves its authentic founder-and-family origin.

### D.legacy — L2

Legacy may inform storytelling without requiring family imagery in every campaign.

### B — L1

Every major SkyyRose collection must have authentic authored meaning grounded in the brand's lived identity, perspective, values, history, or evolving story. Autobiography is a major source of that meaning but is not the only permissible source. A collection must not be reduced to an arbitrary aesthetic, trend, or visual concept without an authentic underlying reason for its existence.

### C — L1

SkyyRose is fundamentally gender-neutral in brand positioning and presentation and is not defined by conventional men's/women's categories. Individual products, people, styling, campaigns, or stories may use gender-specific context when intentionally appropriate, but that context must not silently redefine the brand or canonical product identity.

### G1 — L2

SkyyRose favors specific, authored, context-rich expression over generic luxury conventions. Minimal, restrained, or abstract executions are valid, but they should remain deliberate and meaningfully connected to the collection, product, brand, place, story, or applicable creative context rather than becoming interchangeable with another luxury label.

### G2 — L2

Major SkyyRose collections should have a coherent authored narrative context rooted in their underlying meaning, but individual executions do not need to tell the entire story. When narrative devices are used—story, atmosphere, symbolism, place, character, sequence, or environmental interaction—they should serve the collection, product, or meaning rather than exist as disconnected spectacle.

### G3 — L2

SkyyRose may express collection identity through environments, spatial storytelling, materials, light, place, symbols, character, or other world-building devices when they meaningfully support the collection. The durable principle is intentional, authored contextualization—not a requirement for literal rooms, 3D scenes, monuments, bridges, thrones, or any particular environment.

### G4 — L2

SkyyRose may separate storytelling from transactional presentation, but the two should remain meaningfully connected. Editorial, cinematic, spatial, symbolic, or environmental experiences should provide an understandable route toward the relevant collection or products when used in a commerce context. Product identity, price, availability, selection, and purchase state remain factual commerce concerns and must not be obscured, fictionalized, or subordinated to storytelling.

### G5 — L2

SkyyRose treats atmosphere, wonder, emotional impact, and spectacle as legitimate creative purposes. Immersive techniques—including animation, 3D, spatial interaction, cinematic transitions, environmental storytelling, and emerging media—do not need to function solely as transactional utilities. However, experiences introducing substantial complexity, interaction cost, performance cost, or technical overhead should earn that complexity through an intentional role in storytelling, emotional impact, product discovery, exploration, brand experience, or another defined purpose. Technical sophistication alone is insufficient justification.

### G6 — L2

SkyyRose may use mystery, exploration, unconventional navigation, delayed revelation, or intentional creative friction when those choices meaningfully serve the experience. Creative work does not need to behave like conventional ecommerce. However, users must not become unintentionally trapped, lose essential orientation, or lose access to critical product and commerce actions. Essential controls and appropriate accessibility, preference, device, and capability fallbacks should remain understandable and recoverable.

### G7 — L2

SkyyRose uses cinematic thinking as a compositional, narrative, and experiential discipline rather than as a mandatory visual effect. Cinematic expression may emerge through framing, sequencing, pacing, scale, tension, reveal, atmosphere, sound, silence, movement, stillness, place, character, product focus, interaction, or spatial experience. No particular grading, darkness, camera movement, film grain, transition style, aspect ratio, 3D technique, or visual effect is inherently required.
SkyyRose should actively explore new forms of fashion shopping, product discovery, storytelling, and interaction that give customers experiences beyond conventional ecommerce patterns. Innovation should create meaningful discovery, emotion, participation, understanding, surprise, or connection between customer, collection, story and product rather than novelty for novelty's sake.
3D rooms, cinematic scrolling, environmental discovery, interactive worlds, spatial commerce, game-like exploration, adaptive storytelling and emerging technologies are creative territories—not permanent templates. Future work should be free to invent materially different shopping experiences when they preserve product truth, brand identity, recoverable control, accessibility and commerce integrity.
A still, bright, minimal, physical, conversational, spatial, playful, documentary, photographic, or yet-uninvented experience may be as authentically SkyyRose as today's animated cinematic environments when it carries intentional direction, authored meaning and compelling customer experience.

### G8 — L2

SkyyRose is expected to evolve creatively rather than repeatedly reproduce previously successful executions. Major campaigns, collection experiences, and significant creative initiatives should introduce materially new creative territory through one or more dimensions such as visual direction, materials, environments, storytelling mechanisms, interaction models, media, technology, composition, product discovery, or shopping experience.
Innovation may combine multiple dimensions when the result is coherent. Existing motifs, technologies, materials, environments, or interaction patterns may be reused when contextually meaningful, but previous success alone is insufficient justification for repetition.
SkyyRose should not optimize itself into sameness. Historical repetition and short-term performance are evidence, not permanent creative boundaries. Performance evidence may inform future decisions but may not automatically redefine Brand DNA, eliminate meaningful experimentation, or permanently narrow creative territory.
