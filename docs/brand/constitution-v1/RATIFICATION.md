## Identity decisions A–D

### A — Oakland-specific authored identity

**Evidence:** E03, E06, E08. **Proposed level:** L1.

> SkyyRose is authentically rooted in its Oakland origin, founder authorship and lived history. Future work must not fictionalize, erase, contradict or replace that origin.

**Consequence/scope:** No requirement to depict Oakland in every campaign. Scope: Master brand.

### B — Collections as autobiography

**Evidence:** E03, E06, E07. **Proposed level:** L1.

> Major SkyyRose collections function as authored chapters of lived experience, memory, relationship or legacy. Their meaning must not be replaced with generic trend language without deliberate owner direction.

**Consequence/scope:** Individual product ads need not explain founder biography. Scope: Major collection identity.

### C — Gender-neutral presentation

**Evidence:** E03, E38. **Proposed level:** L1.

> SkyyRose presents its core fashion identity without unsupported gender segmentation while preserving exact canonical product facts and fit information.

**Consequence/scope:** Does not rename products or suppress factual fit details. Scope: Core fashion presentation.

### D — Family and legacy

**Evidence:** E03, E11, E38. **Proposed level:** L1 origin + L2 optional narrative.

> SkyyRose preserves its authentic founder-and-family origin; legacy may inform storytelling without requiring family imagery in every campaign.

**Consequence/scope:** Owner may select L1, L2, L3 or a combination. Scope: Origin and optional storytelling.

## S — Saturation and novelty

> Repetition can create recognition, but devices do not become mandatory because they dominate recent work. Similarity to past creative is not a universal brand-correctness gate.

Proposed statuses:

- **REQUIRED:** Identity depends on it in specified scope.
- **PREFERRED:** Strong current language, not mandatory.
- **AVAILABLE:** Valid but optional.
- **ROTATE:** Valid but currently overrepresented.
- **SCOPED:** Only in named contexts.
- **RETIRED:** No reuse without new approval.

Frequency or similarity alone cannot assign REQUIRED or RETIRED. Existing retirement decisions remain binding. Device assignments need scope; approval of the vocabulary does not automatically retire current scenes or demand a novelty quota.
